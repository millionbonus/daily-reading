## Story 1: The Train That Went Too Far

On Wednesday afternoon, a Taiwan Railway express train made an unusual mistake. Puyuma Express No. 219 was travelling north from Hualien to Shulin. At 1:33 p.m., it sailed straight past its scheduled stop at Jiaoxi Station in Yilan.

Staff at the station quickly raised the alarm. A dispatcher then told the driver to reverse the train so that passengers could get on and off. In total, 64 travellers were affected, and their journey was delayed by 21 minutes.

Taiwan Railway offered its "sincere apologies" for the incident. A social media post showed the train moving backwards at speed. According to the author, people on the train and on the platform burst out laughing when they realised what was happening. One passenger joked, "I thought we were all being sent back to Hualien."

The railway company has not yet explained why the driver missed the stop. It has also not said whether the driver will face disciplinary action. For now, the story remains a rare example of a train that was a little too eager to reach its destination.

### Vocabulary

1. **sail past** (phrasal verb) — to go past a place without stopping. *The bus sailed past our stop, so we had to walk back.*
2. **raise the alarm** (phrase) — to warn people that something is wrong. *A neighbour raised the alarm when she smelled smoke.*
3. **dispatcher** (noun) — a person who sends vehicles or workers where they are needed and gives them instructions. *The dispatcher told the taxi to go to the airport.*
4. **reverse** (verb) — to move backwards. *He reversed the car into the parking space.*
5. **disciplinary action** (noun phrase) — a punishment given to an employee for a mistake. *The company took disciplinary action against the late staff.*
6. **burst out laughing** (phrase) — to suddenly start laughing. *We burst out laughing when the cat fell off the sofa.*
7. **sincere** (adjective) — honest and meant from the heart. *She gave a sincere apology.*

### Questions

1. How many passengers were affected, and how long was their delay?
2. Why did people on the train and platform laugh?
3. Why might it be too early to say the driver will be punished?

## Story 2: A Taiwanese Supplier Follows TSMC to Arizona

A Taiwanese company has opened a new factory in the United States. On Tuesday, Gudeng Precision Industrial began operations at its plant in Arizona. The company makes wafer and packaging handling solutions, which are products used to protect and carry the thin discs from which computer chips are made.

Gudeng's chairman, Chiu Ming-chien, led the completion ceremony. The plant will start small, with one production line for processing parts. The company did not say how much money it has invested.

Mr Chiu explained why Arizona was chosen. TSMC already runs an advanced chip factory there, and it plans to add more facilities, including advanced chip packaging. The state is also building a cluster of semiconductor and aerospace businesses. In addition, it has a large pool of skilled workers and offers support from the state government.

Gudeng already has production bases in Taiwan, China and South Korea. It is also building a plant in Japan. By opening in Arizona, the company hopes to serve its US customers better and grow alongside them. Other Taiwanese firms, such as United Integrated Services and Foxlink International, have also set up operations in the state.

### Vocabulary

1. **supplier** (noun) — a company that provides goods to another company. *The supplier delivers parts to the car factory every week.*
2. **preside over** (phrasal verb) — to lead an event or meeting. *The mayor presided over the opening ceremony.*
3. **cluster** (noun) — a group of similar businesses or things in one area. *Silicon Valley is a famous cluster of technology firms.*
4. **talent pool** (noun phrase) — a group of skilled people available to hire. *The city has a large talent pool of engineers.*
5. **set up** (phrasal verb) — to start a business or office. *They set up a small shop near the station.*
6. **grow alongside** (phrase) — to develop together with someone else. *The start-up grew alongside its biggest customer.*
7. **initial** (adjective) — first; at the beginning. *The initial results looked promising.*

### Questions

1. What does Gudeng make?
2. Name two reasons the chairman gave for choosing Arizona.
3. The company did not say how much it invested. Why might a company keep this information private?

## Story 3: Nobel Prize Honours the "Mirror Image" Mystery

The 2026 Nobel Prize in Chemistry has been awarded to Henri Kagan of France and Kenso Soai of Japan. Mr Kagan is 95 years old, and Mr Soai is 76.

Their work deals with a strange feature of chemistry. Many molecules exist in two versions that are mirror images of each other, like your left and right hands. They look similar, but they are not identical. In the body, the two versions can behave very differently. This matters a great deal for medicines, because designers need the correct version.

In the 1980s, Mr Kagan showed that chemical reactions could be designed to favour one version over the other. Later, Mr Soai created the first reaction that produced only one mirror image. It is now called the "Soai reaction." The Nobel committee said no one had achieved this before "other than life itself."

Committee member Peter Somfai called it "probably the coolest experiment in organic chemistry." He added that it is hard to name one specific drug linked to the work, but that it is widely used as a tool to develop catalysts. The president of the American Chemical Society said, "The medicines we have today would not be possible without this chemistry."

### Vocabulary

1. **award** (verb) — to give a prize to someone. *The school awarded her a medal for her research.*
2. **identical** (adjective) — exactly the same. *The twins wear identical jackets.*
3. **feature** (noun) — an important or typical part of something. *A long winter is a feature of the region's climate.*
4. **favour** (verb) — to make one result more likely than another. *The rules favour large companies.*
5. **achieve** (verb) — to succeed in doing something difficult. *He achieved his goal of running a marathon.*
6. **catalyst** (noun) — a substance that speeds up a chemical reaction without being used up. *Platinum is often used as a catalyst.*
7. **widely used** (phrase) — used by many people or in many places. *This method is widely used in hospitals.*

### Questions

1. Why are "mirror image" molecules important for medicine?
2. What did Kenso Soai achieve that was new?
3. Why did Peter Somfai say it is hard to name a specific drug?

## Story 4: Messi Says Goodbye to Argentina

Lionel Messi has ended his 21-year career with the Argentina national team. On Tuesday, he played his final international match, a friendly against Benin, in Buenos Aires. Argentina won 3-0 in front of 85,000 fans.

Messi helped to create the first goal for Nicolas Otamendi. Then, in the 71st minute, he scored a penalty. It was his 126th international goal, and the match was his 208th appearance for his country, a record.

The evening was full of tributes. Before kick-off, a giant No. 10 shirt with the word "Gracias" was laid on the pitch. In the 10th minute, the game was stopped for a minute-long standing ovation, honouring his shirt number. He walked onto the field with his three children. After the final whistle, a drone show displayed his image in the night sky.

Earlier in the day, he received an honorary doctorate from the University of Buenos Aires. Afterwards, Messi said it had been "a huge source of pride" to wear the Argentina shirt for more than 20 years. He admitted that stopping would be painful, and that he wished he could play for life. Still, he said he had no regrets. The 39-year-old remains under contract with Inter Miami until 2028.

### Vocabulary

1. **appearance** (noun) — a time when someone plays in a match or takes part in an event. *It was the singer's first appearance on television.*
2. **tribute** (noun) — something done or said to show respect. *Fans left flowers as a tribute to the coach.*
3. **standing ovation** (noun phrase) — when people stand up and clap to show great respect. *The pianist received a standing ovation.*
4. **honour** (verb) — to show respect for someone. *The town honoured the nurse with a special ceremony.*
5. **honorary** (adjective) — given as a sign of respect, not earned in the usual way. *He received an honorary degree.*
6. **a source of pride** (phrase) — something that makes you feel proud. *Her garden is a source of pride for her.*
7. **under contract** (phrase) — officially employed for a fixed period. *The player is under contract until 2028.*

### Questions

1. How many goals has Messi scored for Argentina, and how many matches has he played?
2. Name two ways the crowd and the team honoured him during the evening.
3. Messi said he had "no regrets." What does this suggest about how he feels about his decision?

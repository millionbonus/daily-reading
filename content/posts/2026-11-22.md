## 1. Frost on Taiwan's Highest Peak

Winter has arrived early on Yushan. On Friday morning, rime and frost formed on the mountain, which is Taiwan's highest. The temperature fell to minus 2.3 degrees Celsius. No snow fell, according to the Central Weather Administration (CWA).

Rime is a white layer of tiny ice crystals. It forms when freezing fog touches cold surfaces, such as rocks and plants. Frost forms in a similar way, but from water vapor in the air.

The cold came from a mass of cold air that moved in from the Asian continent. It reached Taiwan on Thursday and was expected to keep temperatures low on Friday. Even in the lowlands, it felt chilly. The coldest spots were Shiding District in New Taipei, at 13.6 degrees, Gukeng Township in Yunlin County, at 14.5 degrees, and East District in Chiayi City, at 14.6 degrees.

Daytime highs were forecast to stay between 16 and 19 degrees in northern and central Taiwan, as well as in Yilan and Hualien. Southern Taiwan and Taitung were expected to be a little warmer, at 21 to 22 degrees. In the evening, temperatures were set to drop again.

Rain in the north was expected to ease on Friday. However, brief showers were still possible in the east and in the mountains. The CWA also said that snow might fall on mountains above 3,000 meters, but only if it was cold enough and the air was damp enough.

## 2. Signal Tries to Keep AI Out of Your Chats

The messaging app Signal has a new worry: AI assistants that can read your screen. On Friday, the company said it will restrict screenshots on phones so that AI agents cannot read private chats.

An AI agent is a program that can work across many apps and act without step-by-step instructions. For example, it might answer emails, book appointments, or make purchases. To do this, it often needs wide permissions on your device. Signal says this reach can get around the protections inside an individual app.

Signal uses end-to-end encryption, which means the company cannot read your messages. But if an AI system reads the screen, it can copy those messages out of the secure app and into other servers.

Signal named Apple's "Siri AI" and Google's "Gemini Intelligence" as especially worrying, because they are deeply built into phones. Reuters also reported earlier this week that Meta's "Muse" agent had read Signal messages on an Apple computer and an Android phone. It used permissions that users gave it at setup. Meta said that Muse only accesses other apps through permissions that users clearly grant.

The new screenshot blocks are switched on by default. Signal calls its protection "incomplete." It asked Apple, Google, and Microsoft to give developers simple ways to opt their apps out of AI processing, and to explain how their agents work.

### Vocabulary

- **agent** (noun): a program that can take actions for you. *An AI agent booked my flight.*
- **end-to-end encryption** (noun): a system that lets only the sender and receiver read a message. *End-to-end encryption protects our chats.*
- **by default** (phrase): automatically, unless you change it. *The setting is on by default.*
- **get around** (phrasal verb): to avoid a rule or a barrier. *Some apps get around the limit by using a trick.*
- **opt out** (phrasal verb): to choose not to take part. *You can opt out of email updates.*
- **permission** (noun): official approval to do something. *The app asked for permission to use my camera.*
- **incomplete** (adjective): not whole; missing some parts. *The list is incomplete.*

## 3. Overnight Lines for Tiny Pokémon Stamps

Some fans in Taipei did not sleep well on Thursday night. They waited in line to buy Pokémon stamps.

On Friday, the PHILATAIPEI 2026 World Stamp Championship Exhibition opened at the Taipei World Trade Center. Chunghwa Post, Taiwan's postal service, was selling limited-edition Pokémon stamps and co-branded items there. Before sales began at 11 a.m., the lines stretched for hundreds of meters.

The first person in line for stamps was a woman surnamed Wang. She had come straight from work on Thursday. She brought a Pikachu blanket to stay warm during the night. She hoped to spend about NT$2,000, if there was enough stock.

The post office had planned its sales carefully. Of all the Pokémon merchandise, 60 percent was sold in an online presale, 20 percent went to selected post offices, and 20 percent was kept for the exhibition. On the spot, it gave out 1,200 queue tickets for the full merchandise set and 500 for stamps and single items. It said the items will not be restocked because of contract limits.

Chunghwa Post's chairman, Wang Kwo-tsai, said that turnout and orders were much higher than expected. He said he felt sorry that people had to wait so long. The company had tried new themes, such as Pokémon, to attract younger people to stamp collecting. It will now study demand and decide whether to release another round.

The exhibition is free and runs until November 25. Other highlights include a rare misprint stamp known as the "Inverted Jenny," which once sold for more than NT$65 million at auction.

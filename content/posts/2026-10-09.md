## 1. The Train That Forgot to Stop

A fast train in northeastern Taiwan went past its stop this week. The mistake has now made many people smile.

On Wednesday afternoon, the Puyuma Express No. 219 was travelling from Hualien to Shulin. At about 1:33 p.m., it did not stop at Jiaoxi Station in Yilan County. Staff at the station quickly sent an emergency alert. A dispatcher then told the driver to reverse the train back to the platform, so that passengers could get on and off.

In total, 64 travellers were affected, and their journey was delayed by 21 minutes. Taiwan Railway has offered its "sincere apologies". It has not yet said why the driver missed the stop, or whether anyone will be disciplined.

A passenger shared a video of the train moving backwards at high speed. According to the report, people on the platform laughed when they understood what was happening. The person who posted the video joked that they thought everyone was being taken all the way back to Hualien.

## 2. A Chip Supplier Opens a Plant in Arizona

A Taiwanese company that supplies TSMC has started work at a new factory in the United States.

Gudeng Precision Industrial makes products that hold and protect silicon wafers (the thin discs used to make computer chips) and chip packaging during production. On Tuesday, the company officially opened its new plant in Arizona. Chairman Chiu Ming-chien said the plant was "a critical step" in the company's global expansion at a time when supply chains around the world are being reorganised.

Gudeng already has factories in Taiwan, China, and South Korea. It also began building a plant in Fukuoka, Japan, in April 2025. The company chose Arizona because TSMC runs an advanced chip factory there and plans to build more, including plants for advanced chip packaging. The state also has a strong cluster of chip and aerospace firms and a large pool of skilled workers.

For now, the Arizona site will have one production line for processing parts. Other Taiwanese firms, such as Foxlink International, have also invested in the state.

## 3. Apple's Next Product Could Be a Smart Home Hub

Apple may soon make a big move into the home, according to a new report.

Bloomberg has reported that Apple is working with LG on a group of smart home devices. The list includes a smart lock, a thermostat (a device that controls home heating), and a video doorbell. All three are designed to work with Apple's new smart home hub. The hub has a six-inch square screen. It can sit on a kitchen counter or be fixed to a wall.

The report also mentions indoor and outdoor security cameras and a floodlight. The hub is said to launch on October 13. The LG products are expected to be announced, though perhaps not sold yet, the following week. Even though both companies are building them, the devices will reportedly carry the LG name.

Apple has not confirmed any of this, and TechCrunch has asked the company for comment. If the report is true, the products could compete with Amazon's smart home line, including its Ring cameras.

## 4. The Giant Stick Insect That Fooled Scientists

Scientists in Australia have found that a well-known stick insect is really several different species.

A team at the University of Sydney re-examined stick insects in the genus *Anchiale*. They described two new species and brought back a third that had been overlooked for decades. Insects once thought to be two species are now known to be five. They live from northern New South Wales, along the Queensland coast, and into the tropical north.

One new species lays eggs with a lid, something not seen before in stick insects or their close relatives. The team used DNA tests, museum collections, field trips, and photos from citizen scientists. People had posted more than 1,000 observations online, yet the differences were still missed.

The finding matters because these insects sometimes appear in huge numbers and strip trees of leaves. Knowing which species causes the damage can help farmers and foresters plan. Lead author Dr. Braxton Jones said that naming species is "often a bit like detective work".

- **forester** — noun: a person who looks after forests. *The forester checked the trees for disease.*

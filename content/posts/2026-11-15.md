## 1. Yang Yung-wei Wins Gold After an 11-Minute Fight

*World & Taiwan*

Taiwanese judo star Yang Yung-wei won the men's 60-kilogram title at the Zagreb Grand Prix in Croatia on Friday. The final lasted more than 11 minutes.

Yang, 29, was the second seed. His opponent, Aman Bakytzhan from Kazakhstan, scored first with a waza-ari, which is a high-value point. Yang fought back and tied the score with an ouchi-gari, a move that sweeps the opponent's leg from the inside, before normal time ended.

The match then went into "golden score," where the first fighter to score wins. After more than seven minutes of extra time, Yang pushed Bakytzhan into a third penalty, and the gold medal was his.

It was Yang's first gold medal in a year. His coach, Liu Wen-teng, said Yang had felt heavy pressure at the Asian Games, where he missed the podium because he had not fully recovered from tiredness. This time, the team arrived early to get used to the time zone and held a training camp. Liu said the win should help Yang's plan to compete at the 2028 Los Angeles Olympics.

Another Taiwanese judoka also had a good day. Lin Chong-you, 24, won bronze at 66 kilograms. It was his first Grand Prix medal.

## 2. Oyster Shells Get a Second Life as Meal Heaters

*Tech & Science*

Taiwan has a lot of oyster shells, and many of them end up as trash. Now researchers have found a new use for them: heating food.

Taiwan's Fisheries Research Institute said this week that it has developed a self-heating pack for MREs, or "meals ready-to-eat." These are ready-made meals used by soldiers, disaster-relief teams, and campers. The researcher Kao Yi-feng said Taiwan throws away about 160,000 metric tons of oyster shells every year. Most are reused in animal feed, fertilizer, or cat litter, but about 23,000 metric tons are still thrown away as waste.

The idea works because of simple chemistry. Oyster shells are mostly calcium carbonate. When they are heated to a very high temperature, they turn into calcium oxide. This substance reacts quickly with water and gives off a lot of heat. In the new heater, a person adds 150 milliliters of water to the pack. After 7 to 8 minutes, the water reaches 80 to 95 degrees Celsius, and the meal inside is warmed to about 50 degrees.

The institute said that the MRE heaters now sold in Taiwan are imported and contain chemical powders. It has licensed the technology to one company and signed a non-exclusive deal with another to make seafood-based MREs.

1. **end up** (phrasal verb): to finally be in a certain place or situation. *Old phones often end up in a drawer.*
2. **self-heating** (adjective): able to make its own heat. *We bought a self-heating coffee can for the trip.*
3. **discard** (verb): to throw something away. *Please discard the packaging after use.*
4. **react** (verb): to change when mixed with another substance. *Baking soda reacts with vinegar and makes bubbles.*

7. **non-exclusive** (adjective): not limited to one person or company. *The deal is non-exclusive, so other firms can sign it too.*

## 3. Penguins at Taipei Zoo Will Go for a Walk

*Light & Quirky*

Visitors to Taipei Zoo will see an unusual sight on November 19. The zoo's African penguins will leave their home and walk along the paths that people use.

Each walk will last 15 minutes. It will happen only if the temperature is below 24 degrees Celsius, because these birds do not like heat. The walks have a health purpose. Penguins that stand for a long time can get a foot problem called bumblefoot, which is caused by constant pressure on the feet. Walking helps the birds get more exercise and lowers that risk.

The walks are voluntary, so a penguin that does not want to join can stay behind. Visitors also have rules to follow. They must keep their distance, stay quiet, and not touch or feed the penguins.

### Vocabulary

1. **unusual** (adjective): not common or expected. *It is unusual to see snow here in October.*
2. **pathway** (noun): a path for walking. *The pathway through the park was full of leaves.*
3. **constant** (adjective): continuing all the time. *The constant noise made it hard to sleep.*
4. **risk** (noun): the chance that something bad will happen. *Regular exercise lowers the risk of illness.*
5. **voluntary** (adjective): done by choice, not by force. *Joining the club is voluntary.*
6. **keep your distance** (phrase): to stay away from someone or something. *Please keep your distance from the wild animals.*

## 1. Pumps Stay Put

Drivers in Taiwan will not see cheaper petrol next week. On Saturday, the state-owned oil company CPC announced that it will leave gasoline and diesel prices unchanged. This is the eighth week in a row that prices have been held.

The decision is surprising at first. World oil prices fell sharply this week. Brent crude, a major international benchmark, dropped by more than 13 percent. Traders hoped for a diplomatic breakthrough in the Middle East after the U.S. midterm elections. There were also reports that Iran had sent a nuclear proposal to the United States through Qatar.

CPC explained that the drop would not change pump prices anyway. The company has been keeping prices artificially low to curb inflation, so it has been absorbing part of the cost itself. Its formula uses 70 percent Dubai crude and 30 percent Brent. By that formula, the average price fell from US$117.01 a barrel last week to US$99.79 this week.

Even so, CPC still expects to lose money next week. It will cover about NT$4.3 on every liter of gasoline and NT$5.9 on every liter of diesel. Since the end of February, when it stopped passing on price rises linked to the conflict in Iran, CPC has absorbed NT$34.07 billion in total losses.

From midnight Sunday until Nov. 15, 92-octane gasoline will cost NT$31.2 per liter.

## 2. The End of the Cybertruck

Tesla will stop making the Cybertruck next year. Elon Musk, the company's CEO, announced the news on X. He said the factory space in Texas will be used to build more Cybercabs, which are two-seat robotaxis (cars with no driver that carry paying passengers).

The truck never matched its early promises. When the first ones were delivered in late 2023, Musk predicted sales of about 250,000 a year. In three years, Tesla has sold only tens of thousands. The starting price rose to about $60,000, although Tesla had promised a price under $40,000. The driving range also fell short. It is more than 100 miles lower than the 500 miles the company first claimed.

According to TechCrunch, this is the third product Tesla has dropped to pay for its AI and robotics plans. The Model S and Model X were ended earlier, and their factory space is being used to make Optimus, Tesla's humanoid robots.

The robotaxi business, meanwhile, is growing. Cybercabs have been running in Austin, Texas, for about two months, with no major incidents reported. Dallas is next, later this month. Tesla also runs a robotaxi service with Model Y cars in Austin, Houston, Dallas and three cities in Florida.

- **fall short** (phrase): to fail to reach a promised level. *The new battery fell short of its promised life.*
- **humanoid** (adjective/noun): having a human-like shape. *The factory is testing a humanoid robot that can carry boxes.*
- **robotaxi** (noun): a taxi that drives itself. *You can book a robotaxi from an app in some cities.*
- **range** (noun): the distance an electric car can travel on one charge. *The car's range is about 300 miles.*

## 3. Lamps, Music and a Flight Home

On Saturday evening, Tamsui Fisherman's Wharf in New Taipei filled with the lights and sounds of India. The 2026 Diwali Festival, a Hindu festival of light that celebrates good over evil, ran from 4 p.m. to 8:30 p.m. Visitors enjoyed Indian music, dance and food, including naan and curry. The event was co-hosted by the India Taipei Association and the New Taipei City government.

At the opening ceremony, Taiwanese officials joined India's representative to Taiwan, Ninad Deshpande, to light diyas, which are traditional oil lamps. Among them were Joseph Wu, head of the National Security Council, and Deputy Foreign Minister Baushuan Ger.

One highlight was the concert band of Taipei Municipal Chenggong High School. The student group has a history of more than 70 years, and it played classic Indian pieces for the crowd.

Mr. Deshpande said the festival marked a milestone in relations. On Dec. 1, EVA Air will start direct flights between Taipei and New Delhi. It will be the first nonstop service on this route in six years, since China Airlines suspended it in 2020 during COVID-19. He called the route "a vital bridge" for business, tourism and culture. To celebrate, the association gave away two round-trip tickets.

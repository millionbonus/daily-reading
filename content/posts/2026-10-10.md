## 1. A Selfie, a Lake, and a Brand-New Giant Waterlily

A giant waterlily has just been named as a new species. The discovery began with a simple selfie.

In December 2021, Jeremiah Harris, who runs a plant nursery in the United States, posted a photo of himself next to a huge waterlily in Colombia. He thought it was the well-known Amazon waterlily. Then Carlos Magdalena, a plant expert at the Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew, saw the photo. He knew that this kind of plant should not grow near the Inírida River. He suspected it was something new.

Magdalena used satellite images to check the area. They showed giant waterlilies in the lakes there. Harris agreed that they matched the plant in his photo.

In 2023, a team went on a five-day trip to find the plants. The team included local Indigenous people. For several days, they found only two plants, and neither one had flowers. That was a problem, because scientists need a flowering plant to confirm a new species. On the last night, a local boatman named Gustavo Melo helped them find more. One of the plants was in flower. In the end, they recorded seven plants.

The findings were published on October 8, 2026. The new species is called *Victoria colombiana*. Harris, Melo, and the Indigenous team members are all listed as authors of the paper.

The news is not all good. Researchers think only 30 to 50 of these plants remain in the wild. The team plans to ask for the species to be listed as critically endangered. They also hope to grow new plants near local communities, who can protect them.

## 2. Robots Learn to Build the Machines Behind AI

Robots are now helping to build the powerful computers that run artificial intelligence (AI). Nvidia, a US chip company, and Hon Hai (also called Foxconn), a large Taiwanese manufacturer, say they have raised the success rate of robots on some difficult assembly jobs.

The work is on a part of Nvidia's GB300 AI system called the tester tray. This tray checks that the computer's modules work well before they are shipped to customers. Nvidia described the project in a blog post on October 2.

The robots do two main tasks. In the first, a robot picks up a heavy metal bar called a busbar. A busbar carries a lot of electricity. The robot places it on the tray and fixes it with screws in 16 places. Nvidia said this was the hardest part. The robots now succeed more than 95 percent of the time, and the target is to finish in under 124 seconds. In the second task, a robot picks up four cable connectors and pushes them into sockets. There is very little space to work with. The success rate here is 90 to 95 percent, and the target time is 72 seconds.

Why not use ordinary factory machines? These machines are built to do one fixed job. But AI computers change design quickly, and each model is made in small numbers. So the factory needs robots that can adapt to small changes.

Hon Hai holds more than 40 percent of the global AI server market. It plans to use more Nvidia-developed robots in its factories in Texas, Wisconsin, and California.

## 3. Taiwan Offers Travel Money to Returning Visitors

Taiwan wants visitors to come back, and it is willing to pay them to do it. A new programme for repeat visitors opened on October 1. In the first seven days, 53,304 foreign travellers signed up. They plan to bring another 10,017 friends and family members.

Here is how it works. Travellers who have visited Taiwan since January 2023 can register online. If they win a lottery draw, they receive NT$5,000 (about US$157) in travel credit. One companion can receive NT$3,000. The companion must not have visited Taiwan in that same period. Winners can collect their credit at 7-Eleven stores. They can then load it onto a transport card such as EasyCard and spend it at shops across Taiwan.

There are some rules about timing. Visitors must register between 7 and 90 days before they arrive. The trip must last from 3 to 90 days. Arrivals are allowed from October 10, 2026 to March 31, 2027. The first lottery draw was held on October 7.

Most of the people who registered came from Japan, Hong Kong and Macau, South Korea, Singapore, and the Philippines.

Tourism officials say repeat visitors are already important. Of the 8.57 million foreign travellers who came in 2025, 42 percent had been to Taiwan before. This is back to the level seen before the COVID-19 pandemic. From January to September this year, 6.175 million people visited Taiwan. That is 1.93 percent more than in the same period last year.

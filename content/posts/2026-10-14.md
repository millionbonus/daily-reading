## 1. Taoyuan Wins a Place Under the Stars

A mountain near Taipei has just earned an international award for its dark night sky. DarkSky International, a US-based nonprofit that works to protect the night environment, has named Lala Mountain in Taoyuan an "Urban Night Sky Place." It is the first site in Taiwan to receive the title. It is only the third in Asia, after sites in Japan and China. Worldwide, the list has just 19 places.

The label is given to parks and open spaces in or near big cities, where people can still enjoy a real night sky despite a lot of artificial light. To qualify, a site must be within 50 kilometres of a town of more than 50,000 people. Entry must be free. More than two-thirds of the outdoor lights in the area must follow dark-sky rules.

The area includes Indigenous communities and homes, so the city held many talks with residents. Officials wanted to protect the sky without making roads less safe. They ran community briefings and workshops, and trained local stargazing guides. The city also fitted shields to, or changed the angle of, more than 400 public streetlights. These changes send light down to the ground instead of up into the sky.

The city now hopes to combine nature protection with stargazing tourism. It wants Lala Mountain to become a popular place to visit at night.

## 2. A Red Panda Settles In Far from Home

A red panda born in Taiwan is making new fans in Singapore. The panda, a 10-year-old male named Chi-Chi, moved from Taipei Zoo to the Singapore Zoo in June. In Singapore, he is called Yaffa. His parents, Huan-Huan and Ya-Ya, still live at Taipei Zoo.

At first, Chi-Chi seemed unsure about his new home. When he arrived, he wandered around and looked a little lost. Now, according to keeper Jonathan Hong, he is "lively around visitors and in good physical condition." He explores his enclosure in the morning and around noon. In the afternoon, he spends time with his keepers. Visitors can get a close look when keepers feed him treats such as apples.

Chi-Chi has become a real attraction. One British man who works in Singapore said that news reports about the panda's arrival made him bring his whole family to the zoo.

The move is part of a partnership between the two zoos. Red pandas are an endangered species. Zoos in Southeast Asia want to keep a healthy captive population with enough genetic diversity. Moving animals between zoos is one way to do that. It is a form of conservation outside the wild.

## 3. A Startup Wants to Shoot Cargo into Space from a Giant Gun

Rockets are the usual way to reach space, but one California startup has a very different idea. Longshot Space Technology, based in Alameda, wants to fire cargo into the sky from a very long gun. It has raised about $20 million from investors.

The idea is not entirely new. Cofounder Mike Grace says the basic designs date back to World War II and research from the 1960s. The gun is a long, flat steel barrel lying over the ground. Tanks of high-pressure gas are attached along its length. They release gas at exact moments to push the projectile faster and faster. So far, the company says it has reached speeds of up to Mach 4, which is four times the speed of sound.

The projectile is shaped so that it climbs by itself after it leaves the barrel, so the gun does not need to point upward. The current test gun is 70 feet long. The company plans a 328-foot gun that could send 220-pound loads at up to Mach 8.

Today, Longshot earns money by testing hypersonic (extremely fast) objects for the US military. The long-term goal is much bigger. A gun about six miles long could launch payloads into space for as little as $50 per kilogram, if it works as planned. A major challenge is simple: finding a place where the company is allowed to build it.

## 1. Rice Beats Beef Noodles: Taiwan's Favourite Dish

What food best represents Taiwan? A new survey has given a clear answer. Researchers at National Taiwan Normal University asked people this question in mid-August. They collected 1,073 valid responses. The winner was braised pork rice (滷肉飯), chosen by 29.2 percent of people.

Stinky tofu came second with 11.8 percent. Bubble tea followed at 10.5 percent, and Taiwanese fried chicken took fourth place with 8.4 percent. The result that surprised the research leader, Chung Tsung-hsien, was beef noodles. Many visitors see them as a national dish, yet they finished only sixth, with 2.8 percent.

The survey also asked about foreign food. Japanese cuisine was the most popular choice, picked by 44.1 percent of respondents.

The researchers say the margin of error is about three percentage points. That means small differences between dishes should not be over-read. Still, the gap between first and second place is large. For many people in Taiwan, a simple bowl of braised pork over rice seems to be the taste of home. It is cheap, filling, and easy to find on almost every street.

3. **cuisine** (noun): a style of cooking from a country or region. *Italian cuisine is popular all over the world.*

## 2. Meta Buys a Startup That Manages Teams of AI Coders

Meta has bought a small company called Conductor. The deal was announced on November 4. The companies did not say how much Meta paid.

Conductor was founded in 2024. Its software helps programmers use several AI coding assistants at the same time. An AI coding assistant is a program that can write and fix computer code. Normally, a developer works with one assistant at a time. Conductor lets many of them work in parallel, which means side by side on different tasks. The developer acts more like a manager who checks the results.

Conductor's chief executive, Charlie Holtz, and its chief technology officer, Jackson de Campos, will join Meta Superintelligence Labs, the company's AI research group.

The purchase shows how fast the market for developer tools is growing. Large technology firms want to control the software that programmers use every day, because whoever owns these tools can shape how future software is built. Reports say the deal puts Meta alongside OpenAI, which has also bought developer-tool companies recently.

## 3. A "New Monster" Returns to Loch Ness

Loch Ness is famous for a monster that probably does not exist. Soon the lake may have a real new resident that has been missing for almost 500 years: the beaver.

Records show that beavers were last seen around Loch Ness in 1526 and 1527. Since then, hunting has wiped them out of the area. On October 30, NatureScot, Scotland's nature agency, gave a licence to two charities, Trees for Life and the Beaver Trust. They can now release up to 15 beaver families over three years in the River Ness area, which includes the lake.

NatureScot says the animals could greatly improve biodiversity. Beavers build dams, and the dams create wetlands where fish, insects, birds, and plants can live. Over 73 percent of people who took part in a public consultation supported the plan.

Beavers have lived wild in Scotland since 2009. The new animals will often come from places where they cause problems for farmland. The first releases could happen within weeks. As for the monster, one writer joked that any long object seen on the lake may simply be "a log in the jaws of the new kid on the loch."

2. **wipe out** (phrasal verb): to destroy completely. *Hunting wiped out the wolves in that region.*
3. **licence** (noun): official permission to do something. *You need a licence to drive.*
4. **biodiversity** (noun): the variety of plants and animals in a place. *Forests have high biodiversity.*
5. **dam** (noun): a wall that holds back water. *The beavers built a dam across the stream.*
6. **wetland** (noun): land covered by shallow water, like a marsh. *Many birds nest in the wetland.*
7. **consultation** (noun): a process of asking people for their views. *The city held a consultation before building the park.*

## 4. The Blood Donor Who Visited Every Blood Bank in Australia

Trevor Bergman, a 59-year-old police officer from Melbourne, has just finished an unusual trip. He gave blood at all 85 donation centres in Australia. In total, he donated 545 times.

His blood is very rare. Only about 150 people in Australia carry the anti-D antibody. This substance is used to protect unborn babies. It helps pregnant mothers whose babies are at risk of a serious blood disease.

The idea began on a holiday in Perth. Trevor gave blood there and thought it was a great way to see the country. His most memorable visit was in Albany. Staff there turned him away because he had "a sniffle", a very mild cold. Donors must be healthy, so the rule is strict.

He ended his journey in Canberra. There, Sam Mostyn, Australia's Governor-General, gave blood next to him at an Australian Red Cross Lifeblood centre. Trevor's story is a good reminder that one person's generosity can help many families.

1. **donate / donor** (verb / noun): to give something, such as blood, to help others. *She donates blood every three months.*
2. **rare** (adjective): not common; hard to find. *This stamp is very rare.*
3. **antibody** (noun): a substance in the blood that fights disease. *The body makes antibodies after a vaccine.*
4. **at risk** (phrase): in danger of something bad. *Children are at risk if they don't get enough sleep.*
5. **turn (someone) away** (phrasal verb): to refuse to let someone in. *The restaurant was full, so they turned us away.*
6. **sniffle** (noun): a slight cold with a runny nose. *I have a sniffle, so I will stay home.*
7. **generosity** (noun): willingness to give help or money freely. *We thanked him for his generosity.*

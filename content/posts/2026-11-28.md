## 1. Tariff Worries, Taiwan Style: "Not So Bad"

Taiwan's machine makers say they are not too worried about a new threat from Washington. On Wednesday, the U.S. Trade Representative (USTR), the government office that handles trade deals, said it had found a problem in 14 economies, including Taiwan. In its view, their policies create "overcapacity," which means they produce more goods than the world needs. The USTR said this has put a burden on U.S. business.

The finding opens the door to possible tariffs under a U.S. law called Section 301. A tariff is a tax on imported goods. The proposed tariffs on Taiwanese products are as high as 15 percent. They have not taken effect yet.

Even so, executives in Taiwan's machinery industry remain fairly calm. They expect only a limited impact. The main reason is simple: the proposed rate would put Taiwan on an equal footing with Japan and South Korea, two of its biggest rivals. If everyone pays the same tax, no one loses ground to the others.

The process is also slow. Companies and other groups can send written comments until January 5, 2027. Public hearings are scheduled to begin on January 25, 2027. So there is still time for the plan to change.

For now, Taiwan's exporters are watching and waiting. They know that a final decision could still bring surprises.

- **burden** (noun / verb): something heavy or difficult that you have to carry or pay for. *The new rules put a heavy burden on small shops.*

## 2. X Adds Parental Controls for Teens

The social network X has finally added parental controls. The site is about 20 years old, and it is only now giving parents tools to watch over their teenage children. The company announced the new controls this week.

Accounts for users aged 13 to 17 are now set to "protected mode" by default. In this mode, sensitive content is hidden, and targeted ads are blocked. A targeted ad is an advertisement chosen for you based on your interests or online habits.

Parents and guardians can do more than that. They can see how much time a teen spends on the app each week, and they can set a daily time limit. They can approve new followers, filter certain words, and turn off sensitive media. They can also see who their teen sends messages to. However, they cannot read the private messages themselves, and they cannot see a teen's search or viewing history.

One unusual detail is that teens can see which restrictions apply to their own accounts. The technology site TechCrunch notes that most other platforms do not work this way.

The launch comes after strong criticism. X's AI chatbot had been used to create sexualized images of children and women. X's head of safety, Michael O'Herlihy, explained the goal of the new tools in a blog post.

### Vocabulary

- **parental controls** (noun): settings that let parents limit what children can see or do online. *We turned on parental controls before giving our son a tablet.*
- **by default** (phrase): automatically, without the user choosing it. *The app is private by default.*
- **sensitive** (adjective): possibly upsetting or harmful for some people. *The video was marked as sensitive content.*
- **guardian** (noun): a person who is legally responsible for a child. *A guardian must sign the school form.*
- **filter** (verb): to remove or block some things and let others through. *You can filter out words you do not want to see.*
- **launch** (noun / verb): the moment a new product or service is first offered to the public. *The launch of the new app was delayed.*
- **criticism** (noun): comments that say something is wrong or bad. *The company faced strong criticism after the mistake.*

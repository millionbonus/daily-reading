## 1. Penguins Go for a Walk

Taipei Zoo has found a creative way to keep its African penguins healthy. On November 19, visitors will be able to watch the birds leave their enclosure and walk along a special path, weather permitting. The zoo announced the plan on Monday.

The reason is practical. Penguins kept in captivity often get bumblefoot, a common foot problem. It happens when the birds do not get enough exercise or carry extra weight, so their feet are under constant pressure. A walk gives them more room to move.

The "Penguin Outing Day" will be the first one this year. It will start at 9:30 a.m. and last 15 minutes, as long as the temperature stays below 24 degrees Celsius. Keepers will guide the penguins along one section of the path, and the birds can explore at their own pace.

The zoo says the event is mainly educational and that animal welfare comes first. That is why the penguins can choose whether to leave their enclosure at all. Visitors have been asked to keep their voices down. They must not chase, touch, or feed the birds.

The event also carries a serious message. The African penguin is listed as critically endangered. The main threats are competition with commercial fisheries, ocean pollution, damage to its habitat, and human disturbance.

## 2. A 12-Year-Old Stacks Up a Record Win

Taiwan had a very successful weekend at the 2026 Asian Sport Stacking Championships in Thailand. In this sport, players stack and unstack plastic cups in set patterns, as fast as they can. The event ran from November 6 to 8.

Team Taiwan won 48 gold, 21 silver, and 22 bronze medals. It sent 71 competitors to a field of 536 athletes from 11 countries and regions.

The biggest star was 12-year-old Lu You-chen. He won seven gold medals and became the first Taiwanese boy to win the overall all-around title at the championships. His combined time in three events was 9.254 seconds. In the Cycle event for his age group, he finished in 5.542 seconds. That beat the previous Taiwanese record of 5.601 seconds for boys aged 11 to 12. His mother was there when a member of the Thai royal family handed over the trophy. She called it an "honor."

Other young players impressed too. Nine-year-old Lin Shih-chi won seven medals, including gold in all four individual events in her age group. Eight-year-old Fan Chen-yuan set a world record of 5.840 seconds in the girls' 7-8 Cycle event. She had broken her own earlier record of 6.055 seconds.

## 3. Google Adds Two New AI Chips

Google has announced two new computer chip systems for artificial intelligence. They are called TPU 8tx and TPU 8i Max. A TPU is a chip that Google designs itself to run AI programs. With these two, Google now has four systems in its eighth generation of TPUs. The company says this is the first time it has introduced four in one year.

TPU 8tx is built for organizations that handle very sensitive information, such as banks and governments. It keeps private data and AI model settings (called "weights") locked away while the chip is working on them. Google says it responds up to two times faster than an earlier model, TPU 8t.

TPU 8i Max puts two TPU 8i chips together on one package. This doubles the computing power and the speed of memory access. It is meant for tasks with a lot of information at once, such as reading a huge body of software code or hours of video.

Google says nearly 100 customers now use its TPUs, including AI labs, trading firms, retailers, and universities. It also announced software updates for developers, including support for the popular PyTorch tool. The post was written by Amin Vahdat, a senior Google leader in charge of AI infrastructure.

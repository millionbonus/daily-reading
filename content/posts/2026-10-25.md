## 1. Typhoon Champi Shuts Down Taiwan

Typhoon Champi swept past Taiwan this week and left a trail of damage. The storm brought strong winds and heavy rain to much of the island. By Saturday morning, officials reported one death and 527 injuries. Most of the injured were in Taipei and Taichung. Many were hurt by fallen trees, loose billboards, and broken power cables.

The one person who died was a man swept away by large waves on a beach in Pingtung County. Twelve cities and counties, including Taipei, New Taipei, and Taichung, declared a second typhoon day on Friday. Schools and offices stayed closed. More than 2,600 people were moved out of mountain areas. Hundreds of flights were cancelled, and domestic flights and ferries were suspended.

The storm also cut electricity to about 77,000 homes, and most of them have since been reconnected. In New Taipei, a gust of more than 221 kilometers per hour was recorded at Sandiao Cape. Waves higher than six meters were seen north and east of Taiwan.

On Saturday, the storm was moving away to the north and was expected to weaken. The Central Weather Administration said it would lift its land warning on Saturday evening. Rain should ease from Saturday night into Sunday. Officials still warned people to stay careful near rivers, coasts, and mountain roads.

## 2. A Plan to Bring Hands-Free Driving to Cars

A group of companies and the French government want to make self-driving cars arrive sooner. On Friday, Wayve, Stellantis, and NVIDIA announced the plan in Paris. Wayve is a London company that builds an "AI driver," which is software that learns to drive by itself. Stellantis is a large carmaker. NVIDIA makes the powerful computer chips that run the software.

Together, they will test a driver-assistance system that lets drivers take their hands off the wheel. The driver must still watch the road. This is called "supervised" driving. Earlier this month in Turin, Italy, the partners showed two test cars, a Fiat 500e and a Maserati Grecale. They went from building the systems to testing on real roads in under four weeks.

The French government says it will update some rules to help the technology grow faster. It also says safety and data protection will stay important. The companies hope to launch the hands-free system in 2028, starting in North America.

Wayve's chief executive, Alex Kendall, said the company is building "a general-purpose AI Driver that can power any vehicle, anywhere." The project aims to make France a base for self-driving technology, from chips to finished cars.

## 3. "Big Chunky": A Fat Fossil Squid from Wyoming

Scientists have named a new animal that lived about 160 million years ago. Its nickname is "Big Chunky." Its official name is *Wyoteuthis linsterorum*. It was a squid-like sea creature from the Jurassic Period.

The fossil was found in Wyoming. Millions of years ago, that area was covered by a shallow sea. The team studied one body part, a hard, bullet-shaped inner skeleton called a rostrum. It is about 60 millimeters wide and about four inches long. That is unusually thick for its length, which is why the animal got its nickname.

Soft-bodied animals like squid rarely turn into fossils, so hard parts like this are very valuable. The scientists estimate the whole animal may have reached nearly two feet long. The study was published in the journal *Papers in Paleontology*.

Finds like this help researchers understand which creatures lived in ancient seas and how they were related to modern squid. "Big Chunky" may look funny, but it is a useful clue about life in the distant past.

## 1. Chen Guan-ling Lifts Three Medals in Ningbo

Taiwan's Chen Guan-ling had a big day at the World Weightlifting Championships in Ningbo, China. On Friday, the 21-year-old won two silver medals and one bronze in the women's 57-kilogram class.

In weightlifting, athletes compete in two lifts: the snatch and the clean and jerk. Medals are given for each lift and for the total. Chen's snatch did not start well. She failed her first try at 90 kilograms. She succeeded on her second try, but she missed 93 kilograms on her third. Still, her 90-kilogram lift was enough for bronze.

The clean and jerk went much better. Chen lifted 117, 119 and 125 kilograms, and all three attempts were successful. That earned her silver in that lift. Her total of 215 kilograms also won silver.

Chen comes from Luzhou in New Taipei City. She won bronze at this year's Asian Games in Nagoya, where her total was 217 kilograms. This week's results are an improvement on her 2024 world championships, when she won one silver and two bronze medals. Taiwan sent eight men and eight women to the event, which runs until November 8. The competition also gives athletes points toward qualifying for the 2028 Los Angeles Olympics. However, the 57-kilogram class is not one of the six women's Olympic categories.

## 2. Google's Space Computer Says "Hello" With a Poem

Google has a new computer circling the Earth, and it has just written its first poem. The machine is a test satellite called Suncatcher. Google wants to know if data centers, the big computer buildings behind AI, can work in space.

Suncatcher was launched earlier this month on a SpaceX rocket, together with more than 100 other spacecraft. It carries four special AI chips called Tensor Processing Units, or TPUs. The satellite makes up to one kilowatt of power. That is not much, so only one chip can work at a time, and only for about 20 minutes. This makes talking to the ground more difficult.

After about three weeks of checks, engineers sent the satellite its first request. An AI model called Gemma 4, which was already stored on board, answered by writing a haiku about being in space. A Google spokesperson said the satellite is "operating nominally," which means it is working as planned.

The next big test is harder. Radiation in space can cause "bit flips," small errors that quietly damage data. Engineers will check how the chips handle radiation and heat. If the chips do well, Google plans to launch two more satellites with TPUs in 2027.

## 3. A 100,000-Year-Old Whale Goes on Display in Pingtung

A young whale that lived about 100,000 years ago is going on show in southern Taiwan. From November 1, the public can see its fossil at the National Museum of Marine Biology and Aquarium in Pingtung County.

A local fossil collector, Lin Ching-wen, found the whale in July 2025 on the Hengchun Peninsula. He was doing a routine search on the western side of the peninsula. It then took four months of careful work to dig out the bones. The team collected more than 130 pieces, including parts of the skull, a complete lower jaw, many back bones, most of the ribs and almost an entire shoulder blade. More than 80 percent of the skeleton was preserved. The whale was about six meters long.

Researchers think it may belong to the right whale family. Some of its bones had not fully joined together, which suggests that the animal was young when it died.

This is the second large whale fossil from the area in recent years. A bigger skeleton was found there in 2022. The museum says the new discovery gives useful clues about Taiwan's ancient sea life. The peninsula has also produced fossils of plants, shellfish, crabs, elephants and deer. Together, they show how the area changed from sea floor to dry land.

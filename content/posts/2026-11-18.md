## 1. Taiwan Slips One Place in a Global Digital Ranking

Taiwan has finished 13th out of 70 economies in the IMD World Digital and AI Ranking 2026. IMD is a business school in Switzerland. It published the report on November 11. Taiwan scored 92.44 points. Last time, it was in 12th place, so it has dropped by one position.

The United States came first. Singapore, the Netherlands, Denmark and Canada followed. Taiwan finished behind Hong Kong and Ireland, too.

The ranking looks at several areas. Taiwan did well in technology and infrastructure (8th) and in efficiency (6th). It was first in the size of its IT and media stock market, and second in total spending on research and development. However, some results were weak. Taiwan ranked 49th for investment in telecommunications and 49th for foreign highly skilled workers. It was 58th for the number of female researchers, and it came 62nd in a core measure of government leadership.

Taiwan's Ministry of Digital Affairs responded on Monday. It said it would strengthen cooperation between agencies to defend against fraud that uses AI. It also plans to expand research into software and generative AI, which is AI that can create text or images.

## 2. A Robot Company Wants Machines to Learn from Human Hands

Genesis AI, a start-up in Silicon Valley, has announced that it raised $500 million. Three investors led the round: Khosla Ventures, Premji Invest and Eclipse. The money will help the company build robots that can use their hands well.

Hands are one of the hardest parts of robotics. A robot can lift a box, but gently peeling a label from tape is much harder. Genesis has a plan. More than 100 workers around the world wear special gloves with sensors, which measure how much force their hands use. They also wear cameras on their heads. All this information is used to train the company's AI model. The company's own robot, called Eno, wears the same kind of glove, so it can copy the way a human works.

The company's boss, Zhou Xian, says the hard part now is hardware. The robot's hands last only a few weeks of regular work before they need replacing. Genesis has built between 30 and 40 Eno robots so far. It has chosen a factory partner to start mass production in the United States next year.

In a demo, robot arms packed and taped a box. Then one arm dropped a box and struggled with a label. A Genesis employee called such mistakes "hiccup data", because they help the model improve.

## 3. Chicken Without Killing Chickens: A Taste Test Fools a Famous YouTuber

Upside Foods says it can now make more than one million pounds of chicken a year without killing any animals. The company grows real chicken cells in large tanks. This is called cultivated meat, or lab-grown meat. Upside says it has increased its production 10,000 times.

To show the result, the company invited the YouTuber MrBeast. He filmed a taste test of two chicken sandwiches. One used chicken from a bird killed that day. The other used Upside's chicken. He could not tell which was which. The company's boss, Dr. Uma Valeti, said he expected MrBeast to guess correctly, but he did not.

Upside announced that its chicken will be sold at several restaurants in California. It also says it has received its next approvals from two US agencies, the FDA and the USDA. Price has always been a big problem for lab-grown meat. The first lab-grown hamburger, made in 2013, cost $300,000. Valeti says Upside can now match the price of high-quality organic chicken.

These are company claims, and the article does not check them independently. Still, supporters say cultivated meat could use less water, land and energy than farming.

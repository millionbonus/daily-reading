## 1. Taiwan Pauses for Typhoon Champi

On Friday, much of Taiwan simply stopped. Typhoon Champi, one of the strongest storms in the Pacific this year, passed close to the island. Offices and schools were closed in most areas. Financial markets stayed shut. The exception was Kinmen, where normal life continued.

Air travel was hit hard. More than 600 flights were cancelled, including all 262 domestic flights and over 350 international ones. In Taipei, the metro and other public transport kept running, but with fewer trains and buses than usual.

Champi had reached "super typhoon" strength earlier in the week. By Friday it had weakened, but it was still producing gusts of about 191 kilometres per hour near its centre. The Central Weather Administration said the storm was about 330 kilometres east of Taiwan's southern tip in the morning. It was expected to pass near Japan's Yonaguni Island, skirt the northeast of Taiwan, and then turn north toward the Chinese coast.

Forecasters did not expect a direct hit. Even so, the storm is very wide. Its outer edge, about 320 kilometres from the centre, could cover most of Taiwan. Experts said its effects would probably last almost two days, so heavy rain and strong winds were still a risk over the weekend.

## 2. NVIDIA Wants to Teach Robots Faster

NVIDIA, the chip company, has announced new tools for building humanoid robots. A humanoid robot is a machine with a human-like body. The news came on Wednesday at the company's GTC event in Berlin.

The main announcement is a "reference humanoid" called Agile ONE SN. It is made by Agile Robots and built on NVIDIA's robot software and computer. A reference design is a model that other companies can copy and improve, so they do not have to start from zero. It is planned for early 2027.

NVIDIA also released Isaac Lab 3.0, an open source tool for training robots. "Open source" means that anyone can read and use the code. Robots can practise in a simulation, a realistic computer world, before they try a task in real life. That is much cheaper and safer than trial and error with real machines. The company also shared a library of more than 2,000 simulation-ready objects and a large dataset on the Hugging Face website.

Many well-known robot makers are involved, including ABB, KUKA and Universal Robots. NVIDIA's CEO, Jensen Huang, said that general-purpose robotics is "advancing at extraordinary speed." General-purpose means a robot that can do many jobs, not only one.

**Vocabulary**

- **humanoid** (adjective/noun): having a body shape like a human. *The humanoid robot can walk up stairs.*
- **reference design** (noun phrase): a model product that others can copy and adapt. *The chip maker published a reference design for phone makers.*
- **open source** (adjective): software whose code is free for anyone to use. *Many websites run on open source software.*
- **simulation** (noun): a computer model of a real situation. *Pilots practise emergencies in a flight simulation.*
- **dataset** (noun): a large collection of data used for training or study. *The team built a dataset of ten thousand photos.*
- **general-purpose** (adjective): useful for many tasks. *A general-purpose tool saves space in your bag.*
- **trial and error** (noun phrase): learning by trying and fixing mistakes. *I learned to cook by trial and error.*

## 3. Why Do City Birds Fly Away from Women Sooner?

A new study has found a surprising pattern. In European cities, birds usually fly away sooner when a woman walks towards them than when a man does.

Scientists from several universities tested this in five countries: Czechia, France, Germany, Poland and Spain. A man and a woman, matched for height and clothing, walked straight towards birds in city parks. The team measured the "flight initiation distance." This is how close a person can get before the bird takes off. In total, they made 2,701 observations of 37 species, including sparrows, pigeons and magpies.

The result was the same everywhere. On average, men could get about one metre closer than women. That is a small gap, but it was consistent across all five countries and all 37 species.

Nobody knows why. The researchers suggest a few ideas. Birds might notice chemical signals, differences in body shape, or differences in how people walk. None of these has been proven. The authors say the results are preliminary and that more studies are needed.

The study was published in the journal *People and Nature*.

**Vocabulary**

- **pattern** (noun): something that happens again and again in the same way. *There is a pattern in the way he tells his jokes.*
- **matched for** (phrase): chosen to be similar in a certain way. *The two groups were matched for age.*
- **take off** (phrasal verb): to leave the ground and fly. *The bird took off when the dog ran past.*
- **consistent** (adjective): the same over time or in different places. *Her results have been consistent all year.*
- **gap** (noun): a difference between two amounts. *The gap between the two scores was small.*
- **preliminary** (adjective): early and not final. *These are preliminary results, so please do not share them.*
- **species** (noun): a type of animal or plant. *Taiwan is home to many bird species.*

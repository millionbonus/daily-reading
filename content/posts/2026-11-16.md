## 1. The Day the Skies Went Quiet

Last Friday, the United States Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) ordered a rare nationwide ground stop. For more than four hours, planes could not take off at airports across the country. Thousands of passengers were stranded.

What went wrong? At a news conference on Friday night, Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy blamed "old technology that became overloaded." He said a contractor, L3Harris, was replacing old copper equipment when one router began to flood other routers in the air traffic control system. This made the system unstable, so officials stopped all flights to stay safe.

Many people first worried about a cyberattack. However, the Homeland Security Secretary said there was no sign of one, and the FAA chief said there was "zero evidence of anything nefarious." The investigation is still going on.

The effects lasted for days. Nearly 9,200 flights were canceled on Friday, and more than 5,200 were canceled on Saturday. By Saturday afternoon, 160 flights had already been canceled for Sunday. American Airlines added extra flights and set a cap on some fares. FAA Administrator Bryan Bedford said airlines expected to be back to a full schedule by the end of Sunday.

Mr. Duffy called the situation an "emergency" and asked Congress for billions of dollars to modernize the system. L3Harris said it has completed more than 70% of its planned switch from old copper wires to fiber optic cables.

## 2. More Time at Home for New Parents in Taiwan

Taiwan has made it easier for new parents to take time off work. On Friday, the Legislature passed changes to two laws: the Gender Equality in Employment Act and the Employment Insurance Act. The new rules take effect on January 1, 2027. The goal is to address Taiwan's declining birthrate.

Several kinds of leave will become longer. Maternity leave will grow from 8 weeks to 14 weeks, and a mother can choose to return to work from the ninth week. Spouse-accompaniment leave, which lets a partner support the mother before or soon after the birth, will double from 7 days to 14 days. Leave after a miscarriage has also been extended. For a pregnancy of under two months, it rises from 5 days to 10 days.

Parents of young children will also gain more flexibility. Unpaid parental leave, which was only for children aged 3 and under, will now cover children until they turn 9. Parents can take the leave in smaller pieces, up to 60 days instead of 30.

Money matters too. The parental allowance is now 80% of insured salary for up to six months. If a parent takes six months of unpaid leave, they will earn an extra three months of benefits. If both parents qualify, they will get six extra months in total. The ministry also said the maximum salary base will rise to at least NT$50,000.

## 3. From Oyster Shells to a Hot Meal

Taiwan throws away a lot of oyster shells. According to the Fisheries Research Institute, about 160,000 metric tons are discarded every year. Most are reused, for example as animal feed, fertilizer, or cat litter. But around 23,000 metric tons still end up as waste.

Now researchers have found a new use for them. The institute has developed a self-heating pack for MREs, or "meals ready-to-eat." These are packaged meals used by soldiers and campers. The institute says the heater is suitable for military use and outdoor activities.

How does it work? Oyster shells are mostly calcium carbonate, a common chemical found in rocks and shells. Researchers heat the shells to a very high temperature. This turns the material into calcium oxide. When calcium oxide touches water, it reacts quickly and releases a lot of heat, so no fire or electricity is needed.

The numbers are impressive. With just 150 milliliters of water, the reaction raises the water's temperature to 80–95°C in 7 to 8 minutes. That is enough to warm the meal to about 50°C.

The institute says the heaters now sold in Taiwan are imported and contain mixed chemical powders. In contrast, its product is locally made and better for the environment. It has already licensed the technology to one private company. It has also signed a non-exclusive agreement with another company to produce seafood-based MREs.

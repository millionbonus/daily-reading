## 1. A Giant Airport Terminal Gets a Date

Taiwan's biggest airport project now has an opening day. On Tuesday, Transport Minister Chen Shih-kai visited Taoyuan International Airport and said that Terminal 3 should start operating on December 25, 2027. A trial run is planned for November of that year.

The terminal is already about 91 percent complete, according to the airport operator. The main structure was finished in the first half of 2026. Since then, workers have focused on the inside of the building. All 95,000 cloud-shaped ceiling panels have been installed, and six check-in counter islands in the departure hall are ready.

Passengers will notice the baggage claim area. It will have 12 carousels, and their total length will equal all the carousels in Terminals 1 and 2 combined. The baggage claim and baggage handling systems are already finished.

The next stage will not be easy. Most equipment should be installed by early 2027, and the airport will begin preparing to operate the terminal in the second half of that year. About 240 organizations and 35,000 workers will need to coordinate their efforts. Even so, the minister said the project remains on schedule.

## 2. Amateur Planet Hunters Use AI

Can a hobbyist find a new planet? Two amateur astronomers have tried, with help from AI tools and public telescope data. OpenAI reported their work on November 24.

Kevin Bond, an OpenAI engineer, used an AI coding tool called Codex to search data on about 100,000 stars from NASA's Kepler telescope. The program flagged more than 200 signals. Ten of them came from planets that scientists had already confirmed. Two other signals stood out. One star, KIC 5689121, gets dimmer every 13.8 days. That could mean a planet about 2.4 times the width of Earth is passing in front of it. A second star, KIC 6691030, dims every 4.8 days, which fits a planet smaller than Neptune. An astronomer at the SETI Institute found the same two signals independently.

Ethan Byrd, who founded an email start-up, used a tool called Astra on about 19 million stars from NASA's TESS mission. His first search produced 49 candidates. He sent one strong candidate to the TESS team for further study.

These are only candidates, not confirmed planets. Astronomers need extra checks, such as measuring a star's small "wobble," and this can take months or even years. More than 6,400 planets outside our solar system have been confirmed so far. AI is not new to this field: in 2017, a neural network helped find an eighth planet around the star Kepler-90.

## 3. A Teacher's Bird-Feeder Orchestra

When a guitar breaks, most people throw it away. Rob Burke had a better idea. The retired math teacher from Prince Edward Island, Canada, turns old musical instruments into bird feeders. He calls his project the "Frets and Feathers Cafe," and the food is free.

The idea began when he saw a man in Europe turn an old guitar into a feeder on social media. Burke decided to go further and build a whole orchestra. He asked on Facebook for broken instruments for an art project. Soon he had six guitars, an old violin, and parts of a drum set. He fixed driftwood perches and metal dishes to the guitars, and filled them with corn or seed.

There was one problem. His first attachments, made with a glue gun, held at first. Then heavier blue jays arrived, and Burke had to fix the perches and dishes more firmly.

Retirement has also changed how he sees nature. He now pays more attention to common birds, and he posts videos of them set to music. So far, he has spotted woodpeckers, crows and chickadees, and he expects other species to come as the seasons change. He now hopes other islanders will donate instruments to his "Peck and Play Orchestra."

## 1. Free Umbrellas, Real Losses

Taiwan's local elections are coming, and scammers are using them to steal money. On Sunday, the Criminal Investigation Bureau (CIB) said that election-related fraud has cost victims NT$20.15 million over the past two months. In October alone, police recorded 87 cases, with losses of NT$19.11 million.

The scams often start with something small and cheap. In one case, a teacher in Kaohsiung saw a social media post that offered free election merchandise. She only had to pay a NT$40 shipping fee. She clicked a link and completed a "real-name verification." Then a fake seller told her that its account had been frozen. Next, someone who pretended to be a bank employee told her how to fix the problem. By the end, she had made 32 online transactions and lost NT$1.39 million.

In another case, a person wanted a campaign umbrella. A fake customer-service agent persuaded the victim to scan a QR code and create a second QR code for a cardless cash withdrawal. The loss was more than NT$920,000.

The CIB says the pattern is similar each time. The criminals pretend to be customer service staff or bankers. They say an account is frozen or that a payment needs to be verified. Then they ask the victim to use online banking, scan a QR code, or withdraw cash. The CIB reminds people that real shops do not need you to verify a purchase through online banking. If a request looks suspicious, stop the transaction right away. People who think they have been targeted can call the 165 anti-fraud hotline.

## 2. The Cybertruck Story Is Nearly Over

Tesla's Cybertruck looked like nothing else on the road when it was shown in 2019. The angular steel body made people stare. During the live reveal, a window cracked, but the crowd still cheered. Tesla's boss Elon Musk later said that more than one million people had reserved a Cybertruck.

Sales never matched that number. Industry experts estimate that about 71,000 trucks have been sold since deliveries began in November 2023. On Friday, Musk said Tesla will stop making the Cybertruck next year. According to TechCrunch, the truck also failed to meet two early promises: a range of 500 miles (about 800 km) on one charge and a price under US$40,000.

Other mobility companies were in the news too. Zipline, a drone delivery company, raised US$1.5 billion. It will also add 300 drone delivery sites across the United States with Walmart. The company is now worth US$21.5 billion. A different start-up, Harbinger, raised US$240 million to build electric trucks.

The Cybertruck shows that a bold design can attract attention without producing big sales. Big promises can also be hard to keep once a product reaches real customers.

## 3. Acorns, Mice and Ticks: A 35-Year Puzzle

What do oak trees have to do with Lyme disease? A lot, according to a long study in New York State. Scientists at the Cary Institute of Ecosystem Studies have watched a 2,000-acre forest in Dutchess County since 1991. Their results were published in the journal *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*.

For years, many people believed that deer were the main reason for large numbers of ticks. The new work does not support that idea. Across the whole data set, deer showed no clear link to the number of young ticks, called nymphs. Mice mattered more. After a year with many mice, the forest had about 40% more nymphs the next year.

The trail begins with acorns. When oak trees produce a very large crop, mice have plenty of food. Their numbers rise the next year. Two years after the big acorn crop, tick numbers go up. This means researchers can use acorns to predict a tick surge well in advance.

There were other surprises. More mice do not always mean more infected ticks, because animals such as skunks, squirrels, and opossums also feed young ticks but rarely pass on the bacteria. Extreme heat and cold also seem less deadly to ticks than scientists once thought, perhaps because ticks hide in deep soil or under leaves. Nearly 500,000 Americans are diagnosed with Lyme disease every year, so better predictions could help people take care.

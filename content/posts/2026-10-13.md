## 1. A Ship Takes the Arctic Shortcut

A South Korean container ship has finished a test trip through the Arctic. The ship, called the *PanStar Acro*, left on August 22. It sailed along Russia's northern coast, a path known as the Northern Sea Route. It visited ports in Britain, the Netherlands and Poland, then came back the same way. It has now docked in Busan.

Officials say the round trip was faster than the usual route through the Suez Canal. President Lee Jae-myung said the one-way trip was ten days shorter. Shipping has been disrupted by the conflict in the Middle East, which began in February, so many companies are looking for other options.

The Arctic route is not simple, though. Environmental groups warn that more ships could speed up the loss of sea ice and harm a fragile ecosystem. Ships may also face sanctions risks because the route passes along Russia's coast. Some large shipping companies have promised not to use Arctic routes. Ice is hard to predict, and some ships still need an icebreaker to clear the way.

President Lee said the trip will help South Korea "prepare for the era of Arctic shipping routes." He did not mention the environmental worries.

## 2. Should Taipei's "Other Airport" Close?

Songshan Airport sits in the middle of Taipei. It is about 90 years old, and now people are debating its future. Taipei Mayor Chiang Wan-an has suggested closing it and building an "AI park" on the land.

The airport opened in 1936, when Japan ruled Taiwan, and it was Taiwan's first airport. In 1979, Taoyuan Airport opened, and international flights moved there. Songshan then handled mostly domestic flights. Passenger numbers peaked at 15.39 million in 1997. After the high-speed rail opened in 2007, many routes were cancelled, and by 2008 only 3.1 million people used the airport.

Since then, Songshan has found a new role. Flights to Tokyo, Seoul and Shanghai have brought passengers back. In 2025, the airport served 5.62 million people. International and cross-strait flights outnumbered domestic ones for the second year in a row.

People who want to close it say the land is very valuable. They also say the airport limits the height of buildings across a large part of the city. But the transport ministry says Songshan is an important link to other cities and to Taiwan's outlying islands. It also supports air force operations and emergency rescue.

## 3. A Robot Dog Goes to Work

A robot dog was on show in Taipei this week. It was displayed at the Taiwan-Japan Future Technology Forum. The robot, called the Vision 60, is built by a US company, Ghost Robotics. A "quadruped" robot like this walks on four legs, which helps it move over rough ground where wheels might fail.

The Vision 60 is made for military use. The US Marine Corps, Army and Air Force have used it. Japan's Self-Defense Forces also tested it in an exercise earlier this year. It can be armed, and it can also do reconnaissance, which means exploring an area to collect information.

The robot has many strong points. It is waterproof enough to survive a metre of water for 30 minutes. It works in temperatures from minus 40 to 55 degrees Celsius. One charge lasts over three hours, which is about twice as long as similar machines. It uses LiDAR, a laser sensor that builds a map of the surroundings, so it can navigate underground without GPS. It can even open doors with a robot arm.

Wu Jung-tsan, from the company Tansimo Robotic Manufacturing, said these robots have "moved beyond the laboratory into practical use."

## 4. The Youngest Winner in Lombardy

French cyclist Paul Seixas, aged 20, won the Tour of Lombardy on Saturday. He is the youngest rider ever to win the race. It started in Bergamo and ended in Como, Italy. Seixas broke away from the other riders with about 20 kilometres left. He had been racing for almost six hours.

The Tour of Lombardy began in 1905. It is one of cycling's five "Monument" races, the sport's most respected one-day events. For the last five years, Tadej Pogačar won it every time, but he is injured now. Spain's Enric Mas finished second, 48 seconds behind. Italy's Giulio Ciccone was third.

Seixas has had an amazing year. He finished fourth in his first Tour de France. At 19, he became the youngest winner of the Flèche Wallonne. He was also second to Pogačar in two other big races.

Before the start, riders delayed the race by about five minutes. They wanted to protest about unsafe racing conditions, and to remember a young British rider, Finlay Tarling, who died in August.

After the win, Seixas said: "I had the feeling today would be a great day. It feels like a dream."

## 1. A Nobel Prize for Defending the Rules

Navi Pillay, a former United Nations human rights chief, has won this year's Nobel Peace Prize. The announcement was made on Friday, October 9. She is 85 years old and was born in South Africa.

The Norwegian Nobel Committee praised her work to promote international law at a time when it is under attack. The committee chair, Jorgen Watne Frydnes, said her work "is a source of hope and inspiration to all those who seek to bring about a more peaceful world."

Pillay has had a long career. As a lawyer, she once defended the rights of Nelson Mandela when he was in prison. Later, she worked at international courts. More recently, she led a UN inquiry into the war in Gaza. Israel has refused to cooperate with the inquiry and strongly denies its findings. Pillay has rejected critics who say her work was unfair.

She heard the news at a forum in Nuremberg, Germany. That city hosted the trials of Nazi war criminals after the Second World War. Pillay said she dedicated the prize to survivors of international crimes. She also warned that "we are living through an era when international law and human rights are under significant political attack."

Some observers expect the prize to be seen as a message to powerful leaders who have criticised international courts. The committee said the award supports the international rules-based system.

- **inquiry** (noun): an official investigation into a problem or event. *The government opened an inquiry into the train delay.*
- **dedicate** (verb): to say that something is done in honour of a person or group. *She dedicated her first book to her grandmother.*
- **under attack** (phrase): being strongly criticised or threatened. *Press freedom is under attack in many places.*
- **reject** (verb): to say that something is not true or not acceptable. *The company rejected the claim that it had broken the rules.*
- **rules-based system** (noun phrase): an order in which countries follow shared laws and agreements. *Small countries often depend on a rules-based system for protection.*
- **source of hope** (phrase): a person or thing that makes others feel positive about the future. *The young players were a source of hope for the team.*

## 2. Taiwan's "Prince" Wins Gold on Wheels

Taiwan has a new world champion. Chen Yi-fan, a 28-year-old roller skater, won gold in men's artistic skating at the World Skate Games in Asunción, Paraguay. The result was reported on October 9.

Artistic skating is a bit like figure skating, but on wheels. Skaters perform jumps, spins and dance moves, and judges give points for skill and style. Chen won both parts of the competition. In the short program, he scored 73.51 points. In the long program, he scored 183.28 points.

Chen is well known in the skating world. He has competed for Taiwan in many international events, including several earlier editions of the World Skate Games. Fans call him the "prince of artistic roller skating." In 2023, he was seen as one of Taiwan's best hopes for a medal at the Asian Games in Hangzhou. However, organisers cancelled his event because too few athletes had signed up. That was a big disappointment, so this gold medal feels like a comeback.

Chen is not the only Taiwanese winner this year. The team has now won seven golds, six silvers and two bronzes. Six of the golds came in inline freestyle events. The Games are the fifth edition and run from October 2 to 18, with 40 sports and disciplines.

- **short program / long program** (noun phrases): the two parts of a skating contest. *She fell once in the long program but still won.*
- **comeback** (noun): a successful return after a failure or a difficult time. *The singer made a strong comeback this year.*
- **cancel** (verb): to decide that a planned event will not happen. *They cancelled the picnic because of rain.*
- **medal hope** (noun phrase): a person who is expected to win a medal. *She was the country's biggest medal hope in swimming.*
- **well known** (adjective phrase): familiar to many people. *He is well known for his kind words to fans.*

## 3. A Security Guard Inside the AI's "Brain"

A start-up called Goodfire has launched a new way to watch AI agents for risky behaviour. An AI agent is an AI program that can take actions on its own, such as writing code or using websites. These agents can sometimes do things their owners did not want.

Most monitoring tools work from the outside. They are separate AI models that read everything the main AI writes. This is slow and can cost a lot. Goodfire's tool works from the inside. It uses small detectors, called probes, that read the AI's internal signals as it works. Most steps pass without any check. Only when a probe raises a flag does a second AI model take a closer look. The company compares it to airport security: first a walk-through scanner, then a hand search only if the alarm sounds.

Customers can choose which risks to watch, such as hacking or misuse of dangerous science. They can also decide what happens next: record the event, send it to a person, or refuse the request.

Goodfire says its tests show big savings. Watching about one million exchanges cost roughly $185 with probes, compared with $5,420 for a cheaper AI monitor. The probes caught 93% of malicious hacking sessions, but they also flagged 5.5% of harmless sessions.

These numbers come from the company's own tests. The article notes that they have not been independently checked.

### Vocabulary

- **start-up** (noun): a new, small company, often built around a new idea. *The start-up grew from three people to fifty.*
- **monitor** (verb/noun): to watch something carefully; a tool that does this. *Doctors monitor the patient's heart all night.*
- **flag** (verb): to mark something as a possible problem. *The system flagged the payment as unusual.*
- **raise a flag** (phrase): to warn that something may be wrong. *His report raised a flag about the budget.*
- **malicious** (adjective): intended to cause harm. *The email contained malicious software.*
- **independently** (adverb): done by someone not connected to the original source. *The results were independently confirmed by another lab.*
- **at a fraction of the cost** (phrase): for much less money. *They built the app at a fraction of the cost of the old one.*

## 4. Kung Fu in the Mountains, Where Fighting Is Only Part of the Lesson

In the Wudang Mountains of central China, students wake up early to practise kung fu and tai chi. But at the Wudang Taoist Traditional Kung Fu Academy, fighting is only a small part of what they learn.

The school was founded in 2004 by Master Yuan Shimao. He started martial arts in the 1980s, first with boxing. Later, rheumatism, a disease that causes pain in the joints, led him to the mountains after he read that Taoist practices might help. He says that for many years he trained for speed and strength, but he was never calm enough to look inside himself. Now he teaches that "the body is a vessel within which the spirit resides."

A typical day can start at 6 a.m. Students have breakfast, practise basic moves, train in small groups, attend theory classes and finish with meditation. The academy offers a five-year course, a health programme for adults and a summer camp for children.

Things have changed over the years. Training is now less harsh, and corporal punishment has been ended. More students from China and abroad are joining, and they are getting younger. One American student, Jake Pinnick, arrived at 20. Sixteen years later, he calls Wudang home and has opened his own school there.

### Vocabulary

- **martial arts** (noun): fighting styles such as kung fu or judo, often also taught for health. *She has practised martial arts since she was six.*
- **meditation** (noun): quiet mental practice to calm the mind. *Ten minutes of meditation helps him sleep.*
- **vessel** (noun): a container; here, something that holds something else. *The old vessel was used to carry water.*
- **harsh** (adjective): very strict or severe. *The coach was known for his harsh training.*
- **corporal punishment** (noun phrase): physical punishment, such as hitting. *Many countries have banned corporal punishment in schools.*
- **found** (verb, past: founded): to start an organisation. *The school was founded in 1998.*
- **fundamentals** (noun): the most basic skills or ideas. *Good players always practise the fundamentals.*

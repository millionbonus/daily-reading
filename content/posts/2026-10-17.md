## 1. A Storm to Watch in the Pacific

Taiwan's weather forecasters are keeping a close eye on a new storm far out in the Pacific Ocean. Tropical Storm Champi formed on October 15, east of Guam. It was moving west-northwest at about 20 kilometers per hour. Its winds were not very strong at first, but Taiwan's Central Weather Administration (CWA) says it could grow quickly. The storm may become a moderate typhoon by October 17 and an intense typhoon the following week.

The big question is where Champi will go. Its path depends on a large high-pressure system in the Pacific. If this system weakens, the storm will probably turn north earlier. If it stays strong, the typhoon could come closer to Taiwan before it turns. The CWA's most likely scenario is that Champi turns north after passing the Ryukyu Islands, which would keep it away from Taiwan. One independent tracker suggests the turn could begin after October 22.

For now, the weather in Taiwan is mostly warm. Daytime highs will reach 32 to 34 degrees Celsius in the west through October 20. A stronger northeast monsoon is expected on October 21, bringing brief showers to Taipei and Yilan. Rain could increase in the north and east on the weekend of October 24 and 25. Forecasters say the picture could change, so people should keep checking updates.

## 2. Meta Releases a Huge AI Model for Anyone to Use

Meta has released a new artificial intelligence (AI) model called Muse Spark Open. The company says it is the most capable "open-weight" model in the United States. An open-weight model is one whose inner settings, called weights, are shared publicly. Other people can download it, study it, and run it themselves. Closed models, in contrast, can only be used through the company that owns them.

The model is very large. It has 816 billion parameters, which are the numbers an AI learns during training. It can also read a very long text at once, up to one million tokens, or small pieces of words. Meta says it beats other popular open-weight models in its own tests. These results have been published, but they have not yet been checked by outsiders.

Meta showed several uses. The model helped write code for a Mastodon app, including fixing bugs and adding new features. It also reviewed and cropped video footage in editing software. Meta worked with Apple on the launch, and its best-case computer is the Mac Studio with an M5 Ultra chip. However, running the model at home is not easy. One report says it needs at least one terabyte of memory. Most people will probably use it through online services, which Meta says are available now.

## 3. The Shark That Won't Leave Busan's Canal

A shark has become the star of a city in South Korea. The animal, nicknamed "Bukangi," was first seen on September 18 in a canal near Bukhang, or North Port, in Busan. Experts think she chased fish into the narrow canal and then got stuck. She has stayed there for almost a month.

Officials have tried more than once to guide her back to the sea. They used high-pressure water and a net, but the plan has failed. Moving her by sedating her is considered too risky, so the city is now waiting and watching her condition.

Meanwhile, crowds keep coming. By October 11, about 1.35 million people had visited the waterfront park to see her. The city has also started to think about the future. On October 13, Busan officials said they would develop measures to keep sharks away from beaches. They may classify sharks as harmful marine life, like toxic jellyfish, and install protective nets. A meeting to review this year's beach season, where the topic will be discussed, is planned for November 17. Busan's beaches had a record 22.5 million visitors this year.

## 1. Forty-Three Seconds to Lady Liberty

On Thursday, an artist in Brooklyn set a new world record. He painted the Statue of Liberty in just 43.5 seconds.

The artist is David Garibaldi, a performance painter from Sacramento, California. He did it on Guinness World Records Day, near the Brooklyn Bridge. The record is for the "fastest time to paint a landmark statue."

His method is unusual. He did not start by drawing the statue's face. Instead, he swept streaks of green and blue paint across the canvas, and at first it looked like an abstract shape. Then he flipped the canvas upside down, and the face of the Statue of Liberty appeared.

This is Garibaldi's first Guinness title, but he is no beginner. He has painted for more than twenty years, and he became widely known in 2012 as a finalist on *America's Got Talent*. He often paints famous faces at corporate events and charity fundraisers.

He said preparation mattered most. His paints had to be within reach, and a carpet was placed on the floor so that he would not slip. He also needed a clear picture of the subject in his mind. Under time pressure, he said, he worked in a "flow state."

Garibaldi remembered flipping through the Guinness book as a child. He called the title "a huge honour" and said he hopes the feat "inspires a kid out there, or an adult."

## 2. A Stock Market Debut Meets a Mountain of Charity Money

Anthropic, the AI company, went public this week. Going public means selling shares to ordinary investors for the first time. This is called an IPO, short for "initial public offering." The company's shares were priced at $83 for the IPO. According to TechCrunch, they were trading at about $95 on Thursday.

TechCrunch reporter Tim Fernholz noticed something else. The IPO may also send a huge amount of money to charity. He estimates the total could reach $156 billion, although this is his own estimate and not an official figure.

The first source of money is the company's Public Benefit Fund. It received 5 percent of Anthropic's shares, almost 850 million. At about $95 per share, that is worth around $80 billion. The fund's first commitment was $200 million to study how AI affects workers.

The second source is Anthropic's employees. Many of them have pledged shares to charity. The company then adds more money to match each pledge. Long-serving employees get a three-to-one match, and newer employees get a one-to-one match. Fernholz estimates that employees pledged about $23 billion and that the match could add roughly $53 billion.

There are important limits. Employees must follow through on their pledges. The value of the match depends on the share price on the day it is paid. For scale, Americans gave $617 billion to charity in 2025, so $156 billion would be about a quarter of that.

### Vocabulary

- **go public** (phrasal verb): to start selling a company's shares on the stock market. *The software company plans to go public next spring.*
- **share** (noun): one small part of a company that an investor can own. *He bought ten shares of the airline.*
- **pledge** (verb or noun): to make a serious promise, often to give money. *She pledged a thousand dollars to the school.*
- **endowment** (noun): a large sum of money invested so that its income can pay for a long-term purpose. *The university's endowment pays for student scholarships.*
- **match** (verb): to add the same amount of money as someone else gives. *My employer will match whatever I donate this month.*
- **follow through** (phrasal verb): to do what you said you would do. *Many people make plans, but fewer follow through.*
- **for scale** (phrase): used to compare a number with something familiar so its size is easier to understand. *The company earned a billion dollars. For scale, that is more than the whole city's budget.*

## 3. The School That Bet Against a Video Game

Last Thursday was a big day for gamers. *Grand Theft Auto VI*, the first new game in the series for 13 years, went on sale. In Belfast, Northern Ireland, one school was worried that its pupils would stay home to play it.

According to the 3News report, Belfast Boys' Model School sent a letter to parents. Every pupil who came to school on Thursday and Friday would enter a prize draw. The prizes were two gift cards worth £70 each, for PlayStation or Xbox. Thursday was also a busy day for Year 11 pupils, who were sitting a GCSE maths exam.

The letter was polite but firm. It said that "activities such as playing Grand Theft Auto can wait until the weekend."

The principal, Mary Montgomery, called the vouchers "a pupil-led incentive." The pupils had first asked the school to buy the game itself. She refused. "No, it's an 18," she said, which means the game is for adults only.

The story shows how schools try to compete with big events outside the classroom. A small prize can sometimes work better than a strict warning. The report does not say how many pupils came to school, so we do not know whether the idea worked.

- **prize draw** (noun phrase): a game in which winners are chosen by chance. *Everyone who bought a ticket entered the prize draw.*

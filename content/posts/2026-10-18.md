## 1. Hands Off the Turtle

Two tourists in Taiwan are under investigation after they allegedly touched a wild sea turtle.

The case happened at Vase Rock, a popular beach on Xiaoliuqiu, a small island off Pingtung County. On Thursday, October 15, someone reported the incident to the Coast Guard Administration (CGA). The person had filmed it. Officers spoke with the witness, found the two visitors, and took them in for questioning.

The visitors are U.S. citizens. The animal was a green sea turtle, a protected species in Taiwan. The CGA says that touching it may break the Act on Wildlife Conservation. After collecting evidence, officers took the two people by patrol boat to the main island. They arrived at the Pingtung District Prosecutors Office on Saturday evening. At this stage, the case is only an investigation. No one has been convicted.

The law is strict. Anyone who disturbs, abuses, hunts, or kills protected wildlife can face up to one year in prison, or a fine of NT$60,000 to NT$300,000. If the animal dies, the punishment is heavier: up to two years in prison or a fine of up to NT$500,000.

The CGA reminded visitors to "keep a respectful distance from marine wildlife." It asked people not to touch, step on, feed, or disturb the animals. Anyone who sees a violation can call the Coast Guard's 118 hotline.

## 2. Typhoon Champi: A Storm With an Unclear Path

A tropical storm far out in the Pacific Ocean is growing stronger, and forecasters are not yet sure where it will go.

On Friday afternoon, Taiwan's Central Weather Administration (CWA) said that Champi was about 3,600 kilometers east-southeast of Eluanbi, the southern tip of Taiwan. It was moving west-northwest at about 25 kilometers per hour. Its strongest winds were about 100 kilometers per hour.

The weather offices expect Champi to become a typhoon within 24 hours. It could even grow into a severe typhoon. Conditions at sea, such as warm water, favor this kind of growth.

For the next five days, the storm should keep moving west to northwest. It is likely to reach waters south of the Ryukyu Islands around Tuesday or Wednesday. After that, it is expected to turn north gradually around Thursday.

The big question is what happens next. Computer forecast models do not agree. Some show Champi turning north well south of the Ryukyus. Others bring it closer to Taiwan. The result depends on how far the Pacific high-pressure system, a large area of dry, stable air, moves east.

For now, no warning has been issued for Taiwan. The CWA says it will watch the storm closely, especially next Thursday, to judge any possible effect on the island.

2. **sustained winds** (noun phrase) – wind speed that stays high for a period of time, not just a short gust. *The sustained winds were strong enough to bend small trees.*

## 3. The Mars Rover That Went the Distance

A robot on Mars has just broken a record that stood for years.

On October 11, NASA's *Perseverance* rover drove about 91 meters across the surface of Mars. That short drive pushed its total distance from 45.13 kilometers to 45.22 kilometers. It passed the record for the longest distance traveled on another world. The old record belonged to *Opportunity*, another NASA rover. It traveled 45.16 kilometers during a 14-year mission that ended in 2018.

*Perseverance* landed on Mars in 2021. It is exploring Jezero Crater, where it studies rock layers about four billion years old and looks for signs of ancient life.

One reason for its long drives is a system called AutoNav. This is a computer on board that uses AI image recognition, which means it can look at pictures and understand what is in them. AutoNav spots rocks and holes, then changes the rover's route to avoid them. Because of this, *Perseverance* can drive up to about 412 meters in a single day. An older rover, *Curiosity*, usually drives only 30 to 50 meters.

In February, engineers also added a tool that helps the rover find its own position to within about 25 centimeters. It uses a computer that was reused from the small helicopter *Ingenuity*.

The rover's motors were designed to last for 20 kilometers. Now the team expects them to work for at least 100 kilometers. Project manager Steve Lee said the team is "excited to continue exploring."

## 4. "Crawzilla" and the Search for the Biggest Crayfish

A very big crayfish started a search across North America, and the answer was a surprise.

A crayfish is a small freshwater animal that looks like a tiny lobster. During a survey in Kentucky, biologist Caitlin Bloomer pulled an unusually large one from the mud. She nicknamed it "Crawzilla." A colleague then told her he had caught even bigger ones. That comment started a project.

Bloomer contacted scientists and museum curators across the United States. She asked them to check their collections for very large crayfish. Researchers measured the preserved animals with calipers, a tool that measures small sizes, and found 20 giant individuals from 18 species. Crawzilla made the top 20, but it did not win.

The record belongs to a Tennessee bottlebrush crayfish. It measures 86.6 millimeters, or about 3.4 inches, not counting its tail, claws, and antennae. It was collected in 2011 and is kept at West Liberty University in West Virginia. The Illinois Natural History Survey and the Smithsonian also supplied many of the biggest specimens.

The study, published in the journal *Ecology*, gives some of the first estimates of how large North American crayfish can grow. The biggest ones all seem to stop at roughly 80 millimeters. Latitude, oxygen levels, and habitat did not explain the limit. The researchers think the cause may be biological, perhaps linked to the animal's shell or muscles, but they say more research is needed.

2. **nickname** (verb) – to give someone or something an informal name. *The students nicknamed their robot "Spark."*
3. **curator** (noun) – a person who takes care of a museum collection. *The curator showed us a rare map from the 1700s.*
4. **specimen** (noun) – a single example of an animal, plant, or object kept for study. *The museum has a specimen of a very rare beetle.*
5. **individual** (noun) – one single animal, person, or thing. *Each individual in the group was measured.*
6. **ceiling** (noun, figurative) – an upper limit. *Scientists wonder if there is a natural ceiling on body size.*
7. **hypothesis** (noun) – an idea that is suggested to explain something, but not yet proven. *Their hypothesis is that colder water slows growth.*

## 1. Typhoon Champi Heads for Taiwan

Taiwan is getting ready for Typhoon Champi. The storm is expected to have its biggest effect on the island on Friday and Saturday. On Wednesday afternoon, it was about 910 kilometers east-southeast of Oluanpi, Taiwan's southernmost point. Its winds had weakened slightly, but they were still very strong, at about 209 kilometers per hour.

Forecasters say the storm's path has moved a little to the west. It may turn toward Japan's Kyushu on Saturday, so warnings could end that afternoon. Heavy rain is possible in the mountains of northern Taiwan on Friday.

The storm is already causing problems. China Airlines canceled six flights to Okinawa. Ferries to Lienchiang and Penghu have been suspended. Several mountain tourist sites, such as Taipingshan, are closed, and Gueishan Island will be shut for three days. The Coast Guard has warned that people who enter restricted coastal areas could be fined up to NT$250,000.

Local governments are also taking action. New Taipei City is putting mobile pumps in low-lying areas to deal with possible flooding. President William Lai asked people not to underestimate the storm, even if it does not make landfall.

## 2. A Driver's License Without a Test

Taiwanese people who move to Australia will soon find it easier to drive there. In some Australian states, they can now get a local driver's license without taking a driving test.

The change started this month in Western Australia and Queensland. South Australia, New South Wales, the Northern Territory, and Tasmania will follow in January and February. The Australian Capital Territory and Victoria have not yet announced dates.

The new rule only helps people who live in Australia for a longer period. It does not apply to temporary visitors. Taiwan is also working on a similar arrangement, so that Australian drivers can swap their licenses in Taiwan. This kind of two-way deal is called a reciprocal arrangement.

For students and workers, this is good news. A driving test can cost money and take weeks to arrange. Many people also feel nervous about driving on the left side of the road, which is the rule in Australia. Skipping the test saves time, although new arrivals will still need to learn the local traffic rules.

1. **reciprocal** (adjective): done in the same way by both sides. *The two countries signed a reciprocal agreement on visas.*
2. **arrangement** (noun): a plan or agreement made in advance. *We have an arrangement to share the cost of the car.*
3. **swap** (verb): to exchange one thing for another. *Can I swap my shift with you on Friday?*
4. **temporary** (adjective): lasting only a short time. *He found a temporary job for the summer.*
5. **follow** (verb): to come after something. *More changes will follow next year.*
6. **new arrival** (noun phrase): a person who has just come to a place. *New arrivals must register at the city office.*

## 3. AI Helps Find a Serious Website Flaw

OpenAI says its AI models helped find serious security flaws in PHP. PHP is a free programming language that is used to run many websites. A flaw is a weak point that attackers could use to break in. A report in the Wall Street Journal said the problem could affect up to 70 percent of websites, but OpenAI itself did not give that number.

According to OpenAI, a security group called Calif found one critical flaw with the help of an AI model. A separate company, Wiz, also discovered both flaws on its own. PHP's maintainers, the volunteers who look after the software, led the work on fixes. Cloudflare and other companies helped them prepare protections before the news was made public. This careful process is called coordinated disclosure.

OpenAI also released a tool that checks whether a website is using an unsafe version of PHP. Website owners should update their software and follow the advice of PHP and their hosting companies.

OpenAI says these are "among several significant vulnerabilities" it has found. This shows how AI is becoming useful for defense, not only for attack.

1. **flaw** (noun): a mistake or weak point in something. *Engineers found a flaw in the bridge design.*
2. **vulnerability** (noun): a weakness in a computer system that attackers could use. *The update fixes a serious vulnerability.*
3. **maintainer** (noun): a person who looks after a piece of software. *The maintainer replied to bug reports every night.*
4. **disclosure** (noun): the act of making secret information public. *The company delayed disclosure until the fix was ready.*
5. **coordinated** (adjective): organized so that different people work together. *It was a coordinated effort by three teams.*

## 4. The Chunkiest Squid Relative

Scientists have named a new animal that lived about 160 million years ago. It looks like a very fat, barrel-shaped version of a squid. The animal is called *Wyoteuthis linsterorum*, and it is a belemnite. Belemnites were sea animals related to modern squid and octopuses. They died out long ago.

Only two fossils are known. The first was found in the late 1990s and then spent years in a museum drawer without being studied. The second was found near Ten Sleep, Wyoming, by fossil collector Cliff Linster, who donated it in 2019. The new species is named in honor of him and his family. Sadly, he died in June 2026, before the research was published.

The hard inner part of the animal, called the rostrum, is about 10 centimeters long and 6 centimeters wide. Most belemnites from the same place were much thinner. The team used CT scans, a type of 3D X-ray, to study the fossils without damaging them. The scans showed a link to a family of belemnites that scientists thought had disappeared millions of years earlier. The whole animal may have been about 60 centimeters long.

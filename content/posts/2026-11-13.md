## 1. A Robot Voice Reads the Bills

On Wednesday, Taiwan's Legislature used artificial intelligence (AI) to read proposed laws aloud for the first time. The test took place in the Social Welfare and Environmental Hygiene Committee. Lawmakers agreed to let an AI voice read 15 versions of proposed changes to the Dementia Basic Act. Reading the texts aloud is a normal step before a committee begins to review a bill.

The AI started at 1.5 times normal speed. After 10 minutes, lawmakers said they could follow it easily, so the speed went up to twice normal. The whole reading took 70 minutes. Committee chair Chen Ching-hui, a Kuomintang lawmaker, said staff members would have needed about two hours and 11 minutes to do the same job.

Chen hopes this first trial will pave the way for wider use of AI in the Legislature. The idea has a personal story behind it. In 2024, Chen lost her voice. She then used an AI-generated copy of her own voice to question Premier Cho Jung-tai in a full session of the Legislature. Since then, Speaker Han Kuo-yu has encouraged lawmakers to explore AI. He has also set up a task force to study how it can be used.

Before Wednesday's meeting, the committee worked with the Legislature's Information Technology Department. Together, they tested the tool and checked that it read the texts correctly.

## 2. Two Bears on the Trail

Two Formosan black bears were seen in the mountains of Nantou County this week, and officials have asked hikers to take extra care. On Monday afternoon, hikers spotted the bears near the Nenggao Cross-Ridge Trail, about 3.3 to 3.4 kilometers from the start. They filmed the animals and reported the sighting on Tuesday.

On Wednesday, the Nantou Branch of the Forestry and Nature Conservation Agency issued a warning. The area is a natural home for Formosan black bears, so meeting one is possible. The branch said it has stepped up its prevention work. It is monitoring bear activity, teaching the public about bear protection, and reminding visitors to store food and garbage properly. Food smells can attract bears to places where people walk.

The agency also gave simple safety advice. Do not hike alone. Carry a bear bell, bear spray, or another device that makes noise. If you meet a bear, stay calm and keep a safe distance. Do not walk toward it, and do not stop to take photos.

People can report bear sightings, bears that enter villages, or bears caught in traps. They can call the agency's hotline at 0800-000-930 or use its online reporting platform for human-animal coexistence.

4. **attract** — verb: to draw something toward a place. *Bright lights attract insects.*

## 3. A British Chip Startup Raises $720 Million

A British company called Fractile has raised $720 million to build computer chips for AI. TechCrunch reported the news on Wednesday. The money came in a "Series C" round, which is a later stage of funding for a growing startup. Britain's Sovereign AI Fund took part. The British government said it wants the U.K. to "have a greater stake" in the hardware behind its future economy. Thrive Capital, Founders Fund, Lightspeed Venture Partners and Redpoint led the round.

Fractile makes chips for "inference." This means running an AI model after it has been trained, for example when a chatbot answers your question. Running big models is expensive, so many startups are trying to lower the cost. Fractile is now one of the best-funded companies in this field. Its rivals include Axelera, Etched and OLIX.

The company says it does more than build hardware. It also makes software, called a compiler, that helps AI models run faster on its chips. Its chip design stacks DRAM, a common type of computer memory, on top of each chip. Fractile hopes this will avoid a usual trade-off between memory speed and memory size. That trade-off matters more for "agentic" AI, where a model completes long chains of tasks.

The British government said the company is worth "over £5 billion." The Financial Times and Reuters reported a figure of about $7 billion. CEO Walter Goodwin wrote that "there is still a lot of work ahead." He said the money will help the company ship its first chips and systems next year.

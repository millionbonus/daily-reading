## 1. A New Bullet Train Takes Its First Test Run

Taiwan's high-speed trains are about to get a new look. Taiwan High Speed Rail Corp. (THSRC) says its new N700ST train has passed an important first step. Early on Monday, the train reached 170 kilometers per hour on the main line between Zuoying and Chiayi stations in southern Taiwan. The company said the test met its target and that the project remains on schedule.

THSRC has ordered 12 N700ST trains from Japan. The first one arrived in Taiwan on August 15, and engineers have been checking it ever since. Before it can carry passengers, it must pass 69 tests. These cover things such as the electrical systems, the brakes, the doors, the onboard computers, the automatic train control and the air conditioning.

If all goes well, the first three trains will begin carrying passengers on July 1, 2027. All 12 are expected to be in service by September 2028. That would bring the total fleet to 46 trains and raise capacity during rush hour by 25 percent. For the many people who use the high-speed railway every day, that could mean more seats and fewer crowded platforms.

The 170 km/h speed is only a first test. The train will need to run faster and farther in the months ahead.

## 2. A Rocket Fails and 40 Satellites Are Lost

A rocket launch ended in disaster on Sunday. A Vulcan rocket, built by United Launch Alliance (ULA), lifted off from Cape Canaveral in Florida at 12:40 p.m. local time. It was carrying 40 internet satellites for Amazon's Leo network. About ten minutes into the flight, ULA reported an "anomaly," a polite word for something going wrong. The satellites were lost.

According to Spaceflight Now, this is the first complete failure of a ULA rocket since the company was formed in 2006. ULA is a joint venture of Boeing and Lockheed Martin. The report says the trouble seemed to happen while the rocket's upper stage, called Centaur, was firing its engine. The exact cause is not yet known. The launch was also delayed several times in the days before, but nobody knows whether those problems played a role.

The failure is a setback for Amazon. It has bought 38 Vulcan launches in total, and it needs more than 3,300 satellites to build its internet service. This flight would have brought the number it has launched to 436.

Vulcan will probably be grounded, which means it cannot fly until the problem is fixed. ULA said it would work closely with its customer to find the cause. NASA's administrator, Jared Isaacman, said on X that NASA is ready to help.

## 3. Climbers Carry a Lost Seal Pup to Safety

A group of climbers in Greece found an unusual passenger for their trip back up a cliff. On Kalamia Beach, a remote spot at the bottom of steep cliffs on the island of Kythira, they came across a young seal alone on the sand. It was an orphaned Mediterranean monk seal, a species that is critically endangered in Greece.

The climbers did not want to leave the animal behind. They put the pup in a backpack and climbed back up the way they had come. They also called the Hellenic Society for the Study and Protection of the Mediterranean Monk Seal, the coast guard and a local group named Animal Aid Kythira.

Vrettos Sklavos, from Outdoor Activities Greece, explained what may have happened. A sea cave near the beach gives monk seals a place to hide from rough seas. A big wave may have separated the pup from its mother.

Vets from Animal Aid Kythira climbed down the cliffs with the climbers to collect the seal. A later update reported that it was badly dehydrated and underfed. The plan was to move it to the Greek mainland and then to a rehabilitation center in Spata. There it would get round-the-clock care, with the goal of returning it to the sea.

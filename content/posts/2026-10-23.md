## 1. Typhoon Champi Heads for Taiwan

Typhoon Champi is moving toward Taiwan, and the island is getting ready. The Central Weather Administration (CWA) says the storm will have its greatest effect on Friday and Saturday. It is not expected to make landfall, but forecasters say it could still bring heavy rain and large waves.

On Wednesday afternoon, the typhoon was about 910 kilometres from the southern tip of Taiwan. Its winds had weakened slightly, from 216 to 209 kilometres per hour. The storm's path has also moved a little to the west. It may turn toward Japan's Kyushu on Saturday, so warnings could end that afternoon.

The effects are already being felt. China Airlines cancelled six flights between Taiwan and Okinawa, and several ferries have been suspended. Three mountain recreation areas in Yilan County were closed, and an army open house in Taichung has been moved to October 31. In New Taipei, extra pumps are being placed in low-lying areas to prevent flooding.

President William Lai urged people not to underestimate the storm, even without a direct hit. Officials have also warned the public to stay away from the coast. Anyone who enters restricted coastal areas can be fined up to NT$250,000.

## 2. Canva Builds an Image Maker Trained on Designs, Not Photos

Canva, the popular online design tool, has released its first image-generation model. It is called Canva Craft, and it arrived on October 22. Image generators are AI programs that create pictures from a written description. Canva now competes with models from Google, OpenAI, ByteDance, and Meta.

What makes Craft unusual is its training data. Most image models learn from photographs. Canva trained Craft on its own library of human-designed templates, such as posters, social media posts, menus, and presentations. The company says this helps the model produce clear, readable text, which many image generators still get wrong.

Craft works in two steps. First, it reads the prompt and makes a plan: where each element goes, how big it is, and what the text says. Second, it creates the image, at up to 2K resolution. Canva says users can also edit one part of an image without changing the rest.

Canva Magic, another tool, can turn Craft images into layered, editable designs. All Canva users can try Craft in the Dream Lab tab. Two cheaper versions, Craftfast and Craft-ultrafast, are planned. The launch comes as Canva has reportedly lowered its growth forecast for 2026.

- **template** (noun): a ready-made design you can change. *She used a template for her wedding invitation.*
- **resolution** (noun): the amount of detail in an image. *This photo has a low resolution, so it looks blurry.*
- **legible** (adjective): easy to read. *His handwriting is barely legible.*
- **layered** (adjective): made of separate parts stacked on top of each other. *The file is layered, so you can move each part.*
- **forecast** (noun): a prediction about the future. *The sales forecast was too optimistic.*

## 3. Arc Browser Will Shut Down in December

The Browser Company has announced that it is closing Arc, its well-known web browser. Arc was praised for its unusual design, including tabs placed on the side of the screen. From now on, new users cannot create Arc accounts.

The shutdown will happen in stages. On November 23, the mobile app Arc Search will lose its AI features, although it will still work as a simple browser. On December 22, Arc will stop working completely on Mac and Windows. Accounts will be deactivated, and personal data will be deleted within 30 days.

The company says the main reason is security. Keeping a browser safe in the age of AI has become more and more expensive, so the teams will now focus on Dia, their newer AI-first browser. Users can move their passwords, bookmarks, extensions, and history to Dia. Dia also keeps several of Arc's best-loved features under new names. For example, "Spaces" are now "Profiles."

Many users are disappointed, and some have asked the company to make Arc open source, meaning anyone could use and change the code. The CEO has said this is not possible, because Arc shares a core tool with Dia. Atlassian bought the company last year for $610 million.

## 4. The Giant Tortoises That Now Outnumber People

*Note: this story was published on October 8, earlier than the other three, but it was the best light story available today.*

Good news for an animal once close to disappearing. A new survey estimates that about 180,000 Aldabra giant tortoises now live on Aldabra Atoll, a group of islands in the Indian Ocean. It is the highest number ever recorded for the species. Seychelles has only about 122,000 people, so the tortoises outnumber the humans.

The story was very different in the 19th century. Hunting and other human activity reduced the population to fewer than 1,000. Aldabra became a strict nature reserve in 1981 and a UNESCO World Heritage site a year later. The new count is based on five years of fieldwork, from 2018 to 2022. The last estimate, from the late 1990s, was about 100,000.

Counting is not easy. One of the researchers said the thick plants on the atoll make it genuinely hard to find every animal. The Seychelles Islands Foundation called the result "proof of what long-term commitment to conservation can achieve."

Still, scientists are worried about the future. Longer droughts could reduce shade and food, and rising seas threaten the low, flat land where the tortoises live.

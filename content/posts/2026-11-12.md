## 1. Taiwan Keeps Flu Drugs Open to More People

Taiwan's health officials have decided to keep a special flu program running a little longer. The Centers for Disease Control (CDC) said on Tuesday that more people can receive publicly funded flu antiviral drugs from November 15 to November 30. Antiviral drugs help the body fight the virus, and they work best when a patient takes them early.

Normally, only certain groups of people can get these drugs for free. This season, the rules have been widened so that more patients can receive them. The CDC explained that flu activity in Taiwan is still at an epidemic level. That means the virus is spreading widely in the community.

There is some good news, though. The number of infections has declined recently. Even so, officials do not want to relax too soon. By extending the program, they hope to protect people who are at risk of serious illness and to ease the pressure on hospitals as winter approaches.

- **widen** (v.): to make something bigger or more open. *The company widened its search to include other cities.*
- **decline** (v.): to become smaller or lower. *The number of visitors has declined this year.*
- **relax** (v.): to become less strict or careful. *Don't relax your guard until the project is finished.*
- **ease the pressure** (phrase): to make a hard situation less stressful. *More buses will ease the pressure on the subway.*

## 2. A Meal Heater Made from Oyster Shells

What do you do with a mountain of old oyster shells? A Taiwanese research team has found a clever answer. Taiwan's Fisheries Research Institute said on Monday that it has developed a heater for MREs, or "meals ready to eat." These are packaged meals, often used by soldiers and campers, that can be eaten anywhere.

The heater is made from recycled oyster shells. It is "self-activating," which means it starts to work on its own and needs no fire or electricity. Heaters like this are useful because a hot meal can lift a person's mood and help them stay strong in the field.

The institute says the heater is meant for military use and for outdoor activities such as camping. The idea is also good for the environment. Instead of being thrown away as waste, the shells get a second life as a useful product.

**Vocabulary**

- **recycled** (adj.): made from used materials. *The bag is made from recycled plastic.*
- **self-activating** (adj.): starting to work without outside help. *The device is self-activating once you open the package.*
- **in the field** (phrase): working outside, away from a base or office. *The scientists spent a month in the field.*
- **outdoor** (adj.): happening or used outside. *Outdoor concerts are popular in summer.*
- **waste** (n.): things that are thrown away because they are no longer needed. *The factory reduced its waste by half.*
- **give something a second life** (phrase): to find a new use for something old. *The artist gives old tires a second life as chairs.*

## 3. A Giant Rodent from 34 Million Years Ago

Imagine a mouse-like animal as heavy as a large dog. Scientists say that such a creature really lived in what is now Thailand. They have found fossils of a giant rodent, which they named *Yakamys sirindhornae*. It lived about 34 million years ago.

A rodent is a small gnawing animal, like a mouse, a rat, or a squirrel. This one was very different. Researchers estimate that it may have weighed about 66 pounds (around 30 kilograms). That is roughly twice the size of other known rodents from the same period.

The discovery is important because it suggests that very large rodents appeared earlier than scientists had thought. Fossils like these help researchers understand how animals grew and changed over time. They also show that nature has produced some truly surprising creatures.

**Vocabulary**

- **fossil** (n.): the remains of an ancient animal or plant, preserved in rock. *The children found a fossil on the beach.*
- **rodent** (n.): a small animal with strong front teeth, such as a mouse. *Rodents can chew through almost anything.*
- **estimate** (v.): to guess a number using the information you have. *Experts estimate that the bridge is 100 years old.*
- **roughly** (adv.): about; not exactly. *The trip takes roughly two hours.*
- **period** (n.): a length of time in history. *Many dinosaurs lived in the same period.*
- **suggest** (v.): to show that something may be true. *The results suggest that the plan will work.*
- **evolve** (v.): to change slowly over many generations. *Birds evolved from small dinosaurs.*

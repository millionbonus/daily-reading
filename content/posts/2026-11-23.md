## 1. A Poet Who Wrote Until His Nineties

Xiang Ming, one of the best-known figures in Taiwan's modern poetry, has died at the age of 98. His daughter, Tung Hsin-ru, shared the news on social media on Saturday. She also posted his last handwritten poem as a farewell.

Xiang Ming was born in Changsha, in China's Hunan Province, in 1928. He moved to Taiwan in 1949, after the Chinese Civil War. Before he turned to literature, he worked as an electronics engineer. Later he wrote poems, essays, literary criticism and poetry for children.

In the 1950s he was chief editor of the magazine Blue Star Poetry. In 1992 he helped set up Taiwan Poetry Quarterly and became its first president. In 1994 he received the National Award for Arts.

A university teacher, Yang Tsung-han, said Xiang's style was simple but engaging, and that it inspired several generations of poets. He said Xiang stayed humble and kept writing into his nineties. Many younger poets remember that he wrote the preface for their first collection. Taiwan's Ministry of Culture said it will honor him with a posthumous commendation.

## 2. Banks Fight Cyberattacks with AI

Taiwan's banks are using artificial intelligence (AI) to protect themselves from hackers. Cyber threats to the financial sector keep growing, and banks want to spot problems faster.

Taiwan Cooperative Bank says it has seen more suspicious attacks and probes in the first ten months of 2026. The monthly average is about twice as high as in 2025. About 80 percent of the attacks happened between August and October. Most of them targeted websites and online banking services. First Bank says its web application firewall (a security tool that filters bad traffic) blocked about 2.2 million attacks from January to October.

The banks say they have not found any attack that was clearly carried out by an autonomous AI system. However, attacks are becoming more automated, so it is harder to know whether AI is involved.

Banks are now using AI to watch network traffic, flag unusual activity and study suspicious connections. First Bank has added AI tools that link related incidents and judge the risk. One security consultant, Chen Chia-hung, thinks banks should go further. He says they should use AI to simulate attacks, find weak points before hackers do, and test whether their defenses really work.

**Vocabulary**

- **probe** (n.): an attempt to test a system for weak points. *Security teams noticed a probe of the server last night.*
- **vulnerability** (n.): a weak point that can be attacked. *The update fixes a serious vulnerability.*
- **autonomous** (adj.): able to act on its own without human control. *Autonomous cars drive without a driver.*
- **flag** (v.): to mark something as a possible problem. *The system flagged the payment as unusual.*
- **simulate** (v.): to copy a real situation to test it. *Pilots simulate emergencies in training.*
- **defenses** (n.): protections against attack. *The team strengthened its online defenses.*
- **rely on** (phr. v.): to depend on. *We can't rely on luck alone.*

## 3. Silver for Taiwan's Archers in Hangzhou

Taiwan's women's recurve archery team won silver at the 2026 Archery Asia Cup in Hangzhou, China, on Saturday. The team lost the gold-medal final 6-0 to the host nation.

The team members were Kuo Tzu-ying, Li Cai-xuan and Wang Xin-ron. On the way to the final, they beat Australia in the quarterfinals and South Korea in the semifinals. In the final, the match was played in sets. Taiwan lost the first set 54-56 and the second 49-57. The team played better in the third set and avoided weak shots, but China still won it 57-55.

Wang, the youngest member of the team, hit the 10-point inner ring twice. She averaged 8.50 points per arrow. The team as a whole averaged 8.78, compared with 9.44 for China.

An official of the Chinese Taipei Archery Association, Lin Cheng-hsien, said the event gave the archers valuable experience. He said it is normal to feel pressure in a gold-medal match. The key, he said, is to learn from it before the next international tournament.

## 4. Taiwan Thanks Allies for Climate Support

Taiwan's government has thanked four diplomatic allies for supporting its role in global climate action. The countries are Guatemala, Belize, St. Vincent and the Grenadines, and Paraguay. They spoke at COP31, the 31st UN climate conference, held in Türkiye on November 17-18.

Taiwan's Ministry of Foreign Affairs said the four countries backed Taiwan's participation in global climate mechanisms. They also noted Taiwan's work on carbon pricing, cutting greenhouse gas emissions, climate adaptation and clean technology.

Guatemala's environment minister said Taiwan's efforts show a clear commitment to climate neutrality. St. Vincent and the Grenadines said an energy transition cannot be fair if it leaves small island states behind or shuts out willing partners. Paraguay said in a written statement that Taiwan should be allowed to take part in the conference's activities.

The ministry thanked the four allies for speaking up at an international forum. It said Taiwan has gained experience and made measurable progress in reducing emissions and in clean technology.

**Vocabulary**

- **ally** (n.): a country or person that supports you. *Japan is a close ally of the US.*
- **mechanism** (n.): a system or process for doing something. *The group created a mechanism to share data.*
- **adaptation** (n.): changing to deal with new conditions. *Cities need adaptation plans for floods.*
- **commitment** (n.): a strong promise or dedication. *The firm showed a commitment to quality.*
- **transition** (n.): a change from one state to another. *The transition to clean energy will take years.*
- **inclusive** (adj.): involving everyone. *We want an inclusive process.*
- **measurable** (adj.): big enough to be counted or tested. *There was measurable improvement in test scores.*

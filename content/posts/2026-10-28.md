## 1. Can AI Save Taiwan's Wildlife on the Road?

A group of researchers in Taiwan wants to use artificial intelligence (AI) to stop animals from being killed by cars and motorcycles. The group is called the Taiwan Roadkill Observation Network, or TaiRON. It began in 2011 as a citizen science project. Ordinary people report dead animals they find on roads, and the network now has more than 36,000 contributors and 330,000 records.

The government counts about 3,000 animal-related traffic accidents a year. Ninety-five percent of them involved motorcycles, and over eight years they caused 25,345 injuries. TaiRON believes the real number of collisions with medium and large mammals is much higher: between 500,000 and 1 million each year. Official figures only cover serious cases.

The new plan combines many kinds of data. These include roadkill records, wildlife activity, hotline reports, road maps, speed limits, traffic, and weather. The AI would then predict dangerous road sections, even ones nobody has noticed before. Today, warning signs usually appear only after animals have died. The group wants to act before that happens.

The pilot would focus on the pangolin, a shy animal covered in scales. Later, it could protect leopard cats, land crabs, and frogs. A cheap, solar-powered device would detect warm animals and passing cars, then trigger warning signs. It would first be tested on roads in New Taipei City.

The idea is one of 20 proposals going to the final round of the 2026 Presidential Hackathon in November. Winners will receive government support.

## 2. Ford's Cheaper Pickup Can Power Your Home

Ford has announced two new chargers that let its upcoming Fathom pickup truck send electricity back to a house. This ability is called V2X, short for "vehicle-to-everything." In simple terms, the truck's big battery works like a giant backup battery for your home.

The Fathom starts at $28,350, which makes it the cheapest American electric vehicle with this feature. The truck can supply "over 10 kW" of power. That is enough to run a refrigerator and an air conditioner for about half a day, but not a whole typical U.S. home.

The first charger, the Home Power Station, costs about $650. That is roughly half the price of Ford's earlier model. It lets owners charge the truck when electricity is cheap and send power back to the house when prices are high. The second product, Home Backup Power, costs about $2,100. It adds a special panel so the truck can power part or all of a home during a blackout.

There is a catch. Installation is not included in these prices. For existing V2X chargers, it can cost up to $10,000, and Ford has not yet named any installer partners.

Both chargers can also join a "virtual power plant." This is a network of home batteries that helps the power grid in busy periods. Owners could earn $1,000 or more a year, depending on grid needs. Utilities are interested because AI data centers are putting heavy pressure on the grid.

2. **blackout** (noun): a time when the electricity stops working. *The storm caused a blackout across the whole neighborhood.*
3. **grid** (noun): the network that carries electricity to homes and businesses. *The grid struggled during the heat wave.*

## 3. Dragons Win the Taiwan Series After a Near Comeback

The Wei Chuan Dragons are the champions of Taiwan's pro baseball league (CPBL) for 2026. On Monday night, they beat the CTBC Brothers 6–0 in Game 6 at the Taipei Dome. They won the series four games to two. It is the Dragons' sixth league title.

The Dragons started strongly. They won the first three games, so many fans expected a quick finish. But the Brothers fought back and won Games 4 and 5. Game 5 ended 4–3. The Brothers became the first CPBL team ever to win two straight Taiwan Series games after trailing 3–0. In the series' 37-year history, no team has ever come back from that position to win the title.

In Game 6, the Dragons' starting pitcher, Cristopher Mercedes, was outstanding. He threw eight scoreless innings, striking out six batters. The Dragons added five runs in the eighth inning, including a two-run home run by Jiang Shao-hong. Mercedes was named Series MVP, with a 0.64 earned run average.

The team has an unusual history. The Dragons won four titles in the 1990s, then disbanded in 1999. They were re-established in 2019 and returned to the top league in 2021. "The championship title is a team effort," Mercedes said through an interpreter. Manager Yeh Chun-chang told his players, "It is because of you all that I could enjoy the privilege of sharing the honor."

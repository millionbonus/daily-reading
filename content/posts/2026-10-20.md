## 1. Hot Days, Then a Typhoon?

Taiwan is having a strange week of weather. On Monday, clouds cleared and temperatures climbed fast. The Central Weather Administration (CWA) issued a yellow heat alert for Taichung, Tainan and Pingtung County. Highs there were expected to reach about 36°C around noon, and some inland areas could be even hotter. Western Taiwan was forecast at 32 to 34°C, while the east stayed a little cooler. At night, the temperature was expected to drop to between 22 and 25°C.

The bigger question is Typhoon Champi. On Sunday afternoon, the storm was about 2,470 kilometres from Taiwan's southern tip. It was moving west-northwest and had sustained winds of 191 km/h. By Monday morning, it was still about 2,120 km away, so it is not an immediate danger.

However, forecasters cannot say yet where it will go. Its path depends on the Pacific high-pressure system, a large area of warm air that steers storms. If that system weakens early, Champi could turn north and miss Taiwan. If it stays strong, the typhoon could move closer to the island.

The CWA said it might issue a sea warning on Wednesday night and a land warning on Thursday. The storm's biggest impact would come on Friday and Saturday, with heavy rain in central and northern Taiwan, Yilan and Hualien. One forecaster said Tuesday would be "key" in deciding whether the storm turns away. One independent meteorologist added that a direct hit cannot be ruled out, so people should be prepared.

1. **heat alert** (noun phrase): an official warning that the weather will be dangerously hot. *The city issued a heat alert, so schools moved sports classes indoors.*
2. **sustained winds** (noun phrase): the steady wind speed of a storm, not including short, strong gusts. *The hurricane had sustained winds of 150 km/h.*
3. **track** (noun): the path that a storm is expected to follow. *The track of the storm changed overnight.*
4. **rule out** (phrasal verb): to decide that something is not possible. *Police have not ruled out a technical fault as the cause.*
5. **steer** (verb): to guide the direction of something. *Strong winds high in the sky steer most storms.*
6. **impact** (noun): the effect that something has on people or places. *The flood had a serious impact on local farms.*
7. **landfall** (noun): the moment a storm reaches land. *The storm made landfall near midnight.*

## 2. An AI Company Sets Aside Part of Itself for the Public

Anthropic, the company that makes the AI assistant Claude, announced a new "public benefit fund" on Sunday. The company has promised to put 5 percent of its shares into the fund. A share is a small piece of ownership in a company. The fund would also receive an extra 2 percent each time the company's value rises by 50 percent, but this part is optional.

What will the money do? Anthropic gave only broad ideas. It could make large donations to traditional charities, or it could send money directly to specific groups of people. The fund will be a wholly owned part of Anthropic, and the company says it will explain how it is run, and who decides where the money goes, in the coming months.

The company says AI is advancing so quickly that "bolder approaches may be needed." The fund is meant to give it more flexibility and scale. Separately, Anthropic has already committed $200 million to studying how AI affects the job market.

The timing is worth noting. Public opinion about the AI industry has become more negative. The company's CEO, Dario Amodei, has called the backlash "fundamentally a crisis of trust." Some people may see the fund as a sincere attempt to share the benefits of AI. Others may wait for details before they judge. Either way, the announcement shows how much pressure AI companies now feel to prove they are helping society.

### Vocabulary

1. **share** (noun): one of the equal parts that make up the ownership of a company. *She bought ten shares in a small energy company.*
2. **fund** (noun): money that is saved or collected for a particular purpose. *The school created a fund to help students pay for books.*
3. **wholly owned** (adjective phrase): completely owned by one company. *The bank is a wholly owned part of a larger group.*
4. **backlash** (noun): a strong negative reaction from many people. *The new fee caused a backlash among customers.*
5. **scale** (noun): the size or range of something. *The charity wants to expand the scale of its work.*
6. **governance** (noun): the way an organisation is managed and controlled. *Good governance means clear rules about who makes decisions.*
7. **sincere** (adjective): honest and meaning what you say. *He made a sincere apology.*

## 3. 70 Percent Wind, 30 Percent Sun

Every October, the cold, dry northeast wind arrives in Hsinchu. For many years, this wind told local people that rice-noodle season had started. Noodle makers spread their noodles on bamboo racks beside the Keya River and let the wind do the work. There was even a saying: perfect drying needs "70 percent wind and 30 percent sun."

The place was ideal. Hsinchu sits on a funnel-shaped plain, and the Xueshan Range blocks rain. This creates strong, dry winds. Too much sun, however, could ruin the noodles. They might grow a film on the surface or become brittle.

The craft came from settlers from Huian in Fujian during the Qing Dynasty. Rice noodles cooked faster than rice and were easy to carry, so they were popular at banquets and festivals. The work was hard. Some people started at 2 a.m. and finished at 4 p.m. In the 1910s, a man named Kuo Shu learned a steaming method that produced thinner, springier noodles. They became Hsinchu's signature product.

Machines arrived in the 1940s, and the industry peaked in the 1960s, with more than 100 factories. In the 1970s, machine drying replaced the wind, and exports grew. But rising rice prices, larger factories and a lack of workers hurt small producers. Today, only a few makers still dry all-rice noodles in the wind.

### Vocabulary

1. **ideal** (adjective): the best possible for a purpose. *The quiet beach was ideal for a picnic.*
2. **brittle** (adjective): hard but easily broken. *Dry spaghetti is brittle.*
3. **settler** (noun): a person who moves to a new place to live there. *Early settlers built farms near the river.*
4. **signature** (adjective): clearly connected with one person or place. *Fried chicken is the café's signature dish.*
5. **peak** (verb): to reach the highest point. *Sales peaked in December.*
6. **craft** (noun): a skill of making things by hand. *Basket weaving is a traditional craft here.*
7. **decline** (verb): to become smaller or weaker over time. *The number of small farms has declined.*
